MPSMA-DEIM with a Multi -branch Progressive Stem and Multi -scale 
Context-aware Attention for UAV Small Object Detection  
Peng Sheng*, Jiwei Fang, Wenhao Jiang, Jun Hu, Zixing Wang 
School of Artificial Intelligence and Big Data, Hefei University, Hefei 230601, Anhui, China 
*Corresponding author: CppJava@163.com 
Abstract 
Small objects in unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV) imagery occupy few pixels and can be difficult to distinguish 
from clutter. MPSMA-DEIM adapts DEIM-D-FINE by combining a Multi -branch Progressive Stem (MPS) with 
Multi-scale Context-aware Attention (MSCA). MPS aligns three paths before fusing them into stride-four features. 
MSCA weights backbone features using pooled context at stride 16 and dilated context at stride 32. On 548 
VisDrone2019 validation images under a local Common Objects in Context (COCO) -style protocol, the 
completed full n configuration achieves 0.2255 average precision (AP) and 0.1539 small -object AP (APs) at the 
fixed round-320 exponential moving average (EMA) endpoint. The corresponding baseline values are 0.1902 and 
0.1215. The full and baseline configurations have 7.54 and 3.74 million trainable parameters, respectively, and 
their checkpoint recovery histories differ. The stem-replacement run records APs of 0.1362. The run with all three 
MSCA gates bypassed records APs of 0.1473 and has 3.84 million fewer trainable parameters than the full 
configuration. All results come from one seed on one validation split. 
Keywords: UAV imagery; small object detection; multi-branch progressive stem; multi-scale context-aware 
attention; DEIM-D-FINE 
1. Introduction 
In unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV) imagery, small objects often occupy few pixels, overlap with neighbours and 
appear against cluttered backgrounds [1], [2]. These conditions motivate examining how local detail and 
surrounding context can inform detection. 
Liang et al. use convolution to augment local features in a Swin Transformer for UAV imagery [3]. DEIM 
introduces dense one -to-one matching and a matchability -aware loss [4], and D -FINE refines bounding -box 
distributions across decoder layers [5]. We use DEIM -D-FINE as the host and modify the backbone to combine 
early detail extraction with contextual weighting. 
MPS aligns main, detail and high -frequency paths, weights their concatenation and projects it to stride -four 
features. MSCA applies two pooled -context gates at stride 16 and one dilated -context gate at stride 32 after HG -
block aggregation. The contribution is their placement and integration in HGNetv2 -B0. The complete 
configuration also uses full -width aggregation projections and the sigmoid -weighted linear unit (SiLU) in 
configurable blocks. We report fixed -round and validation -selected accuracy for the complete n configuration, 
assess two conditional removals and quantify the accompanying parameter changes. 
2. Related Work 
YOLOv10 uses consistent dual assignments to support inference without non -maximum suppression (NMS) [6]. 
DEIM [4] and D -FINE [5] address matching and localization in detection transformers. MPSMA -DEIM uses the 
encoder and decoder of DEIM -D-FINE and modifies the backbone. The evaluation measures detection accuracy 
and trainable parameter count. 
Effective squeeze -excitation (ESE) in VoVNetV2 recalibrates channels [7]. SimAM computes neuron -wise 
weights from an energy function without adding learned parameters [8]. DMF -Net pairs multi -branch detail 
extraction with dual-attention fusion [9]. TF-DEIM-DFINE uses Mamba, self-attention and a feature pyramid [10]. 
In MPSMA-DEIM, multi-branch fusion takes place in the input stem. The backbone gates apply SimAM -style 
weighting to pooled or learned context and use the resulting weights to modulate the original feature maps. 



3. Proposed Method 
3.1 Overall Architecture 
MPSMA-DEIM uses an HGNetv2 -B0 backbone, a hybrid feature encoder and a D -FINE decoder trained within 
the DEIM framework [4,5]. Figure 1 shows the implemented n and s configurations. Both use the same B0 stage 
widths and modified backbone. The evaluated n configuration supplies stride -16 and stride -32 features to an 
encoder of width 128. The encoder has one eight -head layer; the three-layer decoder uses 300 regular queries and 
six sampling points per feature level. The implementation also supports an s configuration with an additional 
stride-8 input and width 256; it is outside the n-model results reported here. MPS and MSCA modify the backbone, 
while the detection losses and decoder design are inherited. 
 
Fig. 1. Backbone and scale-dependent encoder inputs at 640 ×  640. Tensor labels use channels ×  height ×  width. The dashed 
connection is present only in the s configuration. 
3.2 Multi-branch Progressive Stem 
MPS replaces the standard stem with three paths (Fig. 2). The B0 detail path applies two stride -one 3 ×  3 blocks 
with 8 channels and 2 ×  2 average pooling with stride two. The main path applies two 16 -channel 3 ×  3 blocks 
with strides two and one. The high -frequency path forms X − AvgPool3×3(X) with stride one and padding one, 
then applies a 1 ×  1 projection and a depthwise 3 ×  3 convolution with 4 channels. Both high -frequency 
convolutions are followed by batch normalization and activation. Bilinear interpolation with align_corners=False 
matches the main path. For an input X with even dimensions, concatenating the main, detail and high -frequency 
outputs in that order gives a 28-channel feature Z at half resolution. 
A 1 ×  1 convolution, batch normalization and activation reduce Z from 28 to 2 channels. A 7 ×  7 convolution with 
padding three and a sigmoid produce the spatial gate A(Z). The weighted feature is Z ·  [1 + A(Z)], 
where ·  denotes element-wise multiplication. Fusion uses 1 ×  1 and 3 ×  3 convolution blocks with channel 
transitions 28 → 32 → 16. A final 16-channel stride-two 3 ×  3 block gives the same stride-four output as the 
original stem. Each ConvBNAct in the detail, main, fusion and final downsampling paths applies a learnable 
affine block (LAB) with scalar scale and bias after activation. The high-frequency and spatial-gate paths omit 
LAB.

3.3 Multi-scale Context-aware Attention 
The modified backbone uses ESE, SimAM, MSCA -Lite and MSCA-Dilated in its four stages (Fig. 3). Each HG 
block concatenates its features, projects them directly to its output width C using one 1 ×  1 ConvBNAct, and then 
applies its gate. The baseline uses two 1 ×  1 ConvBNAct blocks with intermediate width C/2 and no separate ESE 
or SimAM gates; the modified backbone also changes this projection path. B0 has one block in stages 1, 2 and 4 
and two in stage 3, giving two MSCA-Lite instances. Stage outputs are 64, 256, 512 and 1024 channels at strides 4, 
8, 16 and 32. Stage -2 SimAM uses variance denominator HW − 1 [8]; the two MSCA variants use denominator 
HW, so we describe them as SimAM-style gates.

×  1 ConvBNAct followed by its gate.

MSCA-Dilated (Fig. 5) uses three branches Bd with dilation d ∈ {1, 2, 4}. Each branch applies a 1 ×  1 
convolution from C to C/8 channels, batch normalization and a rectified linear unit (ReLU). A grouped 3 ×  3 
convolution with C/8 groups expands C/8 channels to C, producing eight outputs per group, and is followed by 
batch normalization. A learned 1 ×  1 projection combines the concatenated branch outputs. The gate uses G from 
Eq. (2) on the projected feature.

A separate channel gate uses global average pooling (GAP) of F and two 1 ×  1 convolutions, reducing C to C/8 
and restoring C. Their weights and biases are W₁, W₂ and b₁, b₂, respectively: 
𝐴𝑐 =  𝑠𝑖𝑔𝑚𝑜𝑖𝑑{𝑊₂ 𝑅𝑒𝐿𝑈[𝑊₁ 𝐺𝐴𝑃(𝐹) +  𝑏₁] +  𝑏₂}.                               (4) 
The output weights the original input by both gates, with the channel gate broadcast over spatial positions: 
𝑌𝑑𝑖𝑙𝑎𝑡𝑒𝑑 =  𝐹 ·  𝐴𝑠 ·  𝐴𝑐.                                                     (5) 
For kernel size k and dilation d, the effective kernel size is 
𝑘𝑒𝑓𝑓 =  𝑘 +  (𝑘 −  1)(𝑑 −  1).                                                  (6) 
With k = 3, the effective sizes are 3, 5 and 9 for d = 1, 2 and 4. All branches use stride one and padding d, 
preserving H ×  W. For stage 4, C = 1024 and the reduced width and group count are both 128. The contextual 
spatial gate remains channel-specific, with shape C ×  H ×  W; it is not a single H ×  W mask. In stage 4, the Dilated 
gate has 3,841,280 trainable parameters; its 3072-to-1024 context projection accounts for 3,146,752.

3.4 Backbone Activation 
The full configuration uses SiLU, f(x) = x sigmoid(x) [11], in the configurable stem and backbone convolution 
blocks. MSCA-Dilated uses ReLU within its contextual branches and channel gate. The original baseline uses 
ReLU in its configurable backbone blocks. Both configurations use the same encoder and decoder activation 
settings. 
4. Experiments 
4.1 Dataset and Evaluation 
We use the VisDrone2019 detection training and validation splits [2], with 6,471 and 548 images, respectively, 
across ten categories. Inputs are resized to 640 ×  640. The frozen converted annotations contain 343,205 training 
boxes and 38,759 validation boxes. All validation images are retained, including two byte -identical images with 
different annotation records. The same data files are used in every arm. 
Evaluation follows a local bounding -box protocol based on Common Objects in Context (COCO) [12]: 
intersection-over-union (IoU) thresholds 0.50 –0.95 in steps of 0.05, 101 recall thresholds, class -aware matching 
and maxDets=[1, 10, 100] per image and category. The postprocessor returns 300 predictions per image across 
categories. Average precision (AP) averages the ten IoU thresholds; AP50 and AP75 use 0.50 and 0.75. Small -
object AP (APs) uses original -image box area up to 1,024 square pixels. Average recall (AR) is reported at 
maxDets=100 as AR@100. Its large -object counterpart, ARl, uses original -image area of at least 9,216 square 
pixels. 
The converted annotations have iscrowd=0 and no ignore field. The 1,378 ignored -region entries and 32 entries 
labelled others in the original validation annotations are absent. The official VisDrone toolkit filters detections 
using ignored regions and uses an AP detection limit of 500 [13]. The local protocol therefore differs from the 
official challenge evaluation, and the reported values are not used to rank against official test-dev results. 
4.2 Training and Endpoint Protocol 
BASE is the original n detector with its standard stem, two -convolution aggregation paths and ReLU backbone 
activations. FULL is the complete n configuration described in Section 3, including its aggregation projections 
and activation choices. Both use seed 0, 32 -bit floating point (FP32) with automatic mixed precision and 
TensorFloat-32 (TF32) disabled, batch size 4, and one NVIDIA RTX 3090 graphics processing unit (GPU) per 
run. The software versions are PyTorch 2.5.1+cu124, CUDA 12.4 and faster-coco-eval 1.8.0. Pretrained-backbone 




loading and tuning are disabled. A predefined set of 554 shared encoder, decoder, stage -downsampling and HG-
block tensors has identical initial values. The first 100 training input batches were checked for exact agreement. 
Each completed run contributes 320 retained rounds of 1,617 optimizer update attempts, for a total of 517,440 
retained attempts per run. The training loader drops its final incomplete batch. Round numbers are one -based. 
AdamW uses betas (0.9, 0.999), base learning rates of 4 × 10⁻⁴ for the backbone and 8 × 10⁻⁴ otherwise, weight 
decay 10⁻⁴, and the frozen zero -decay normalization and encoder/decoder bias groups. The gradient norm is 
clipped at 0.1. The first update uses the base rate divided by 500. The rate is set after each update, with quadratic 
warm-up over 2,000 steps and a constant rate thereafter because lr_gamma=1.0. The exponential moving average 
(EMA) uses decay 0.9999 and a ramp time constant of 1,000 updates. 
Mosaic and batch MixUp each have selection probability 0.5 in rounds 5 –156. Photometric distortion, zoom -out 
and IoU cropping are eligible in rounds 5 –296; Mosaic-selected samples skip zoom -out and IoU cropping. From 
round 297, these strong augmentations are disabled, while horizontal flipping and resizing remain. Batch multi -
scale resizing is disabled. The native late-stage policy restores the best-AP checkpoint from the first 296 rounds at 
stage entry and after a later round that does not set a new global best. This policy is identical in all arms. 
The primary endpoint is the EMA evaluated at actual round 320 before any subsequent restore. The secondary 
endpoint is the highest-AP round within each run, choosing the earliest round on ties. APs and all other metrics 
follow that same AP -selected checkpoint. Differences are computed from unrounded metrics. Stored endpoint 
weights, prediction files, evaluator arrays and metric rows are bound by hashes and were checked together.

4.4 Conditional Module Removal 
The two ablations use the same data, optimizer, augmentation, evaluation and 320 -round schedule as FULL. 
NO_MPS replaces MPS with the original BASE stem configured with SiLU and keeps the other FULL 
components. NO_MSCA bypasses both MSCA -Lite gates and the MSCA -Dilated gate while retaining the 
aggregation projections, ESE, SimAM, MPS and SiLU. The retained tensors use their FULL initial values, and the 
replacement stem uses the original BASE stem tensors.

4.5 Limitations 
The results use one seed and one validation split. The same split guides late -stage restoration and selection of the 
best-AP checkpoint, so both endpoints describe validation performance. Run -to-run variability, independent test 
performance, latency and onboard performance remain unmeasured. 
BASE resumed from the round -214 checkpoint after the interrupted branch had logged 225 rounds. The retained 
trajectory uses the rerun records for rounds 215–225. FULL resumed after round 1. Both checkpoints lacked main-
process random states, which were reinitialized with seed 0. BASE resumed again after round 308 with saved 
random states and reran part of round 309. NO_MPS began from its initial weights. The FULL per-batch learning-
rate record starts at round 2. 
NO_MSCA resumed from complete checkpoints after rounds 29 and 164 on replacement RTX 3090 instances, 
restoring model and EMA weights, optimizer and warmup state, and all saved random states. Partial work in 
round 165 was discarded after the second interruption. The full amount of unretained work across the interruptions 
is unknown; retained round counts therefore do not establish equal physical training computation or bitwise 
equivalence to uninterrupted training. 
5. Conclusion 
MPSMA-DEIM integrates a three-path stride-four stem and contextual gates into the DEIM-D-FINE backbone. At 
the fixed round-320 EMA endpoint, the completed full configuration achieves AP 0.2255 and APs 0.1539 on the 
local VisDrone2019 validation split, with 2.02 times the trainable parameters of BASE. The stem comparison 
shows higher APs and lower large -object recall for FULL. The MSCA comparison couples a smaller APs 
difference with a 3.84 million parameter change. The results describe single -seed runs with different checkpoint 
histories. The effects of individual gates, the contribution of model capacity and deployment performance remain 
open.
