Abstract
Detecting pedestrians in UAV (Unmanned Aerial Vehicle)
imagery poses several challenges due to factors such as sig-
nificant scale variation, low resolution, dense crowds, and
cluttered backgrounds. In aerial views, pedestrians often
occupy only a few pixels, making them difficult to detect
using standard object detection architectures that rely on
high-level feature maps. Most modern detectors begin pre-
dictions at a stride of 8 resolutions, which limits their abil-
ity to detect extremely small objects. In this study, we revisit
the feature pyramid architecture of YOLOv12 and introduce
a high-resolution P2 detection head to improve supervision
at the early stages of the network. Our proposed modifica-
tion extends the pyramid to a stride of 4 resolutions and in-
corporates bidirectional feature refinement to maintain se-
mantic consistency across different scales. This design re-
mains lightweight and preserves practical inference speed
while improving the representation of tiny objects. We eval-
uate our approach on two aerial pedestrian benchmarks:
VisDrone and TinyPerson. The proposed model improves
the mean Average Precision (mAP) at IoU 0.5 from 0.63 to
0.69, which represents a 9.5% relative gain on the VisDrone
dataset. On the TinyPerson dataset, mAP@0.5 increases
from 0.40 to 0.45, indicating a 12.5% relative gain. Addi-
tionally, there is a 25% relative increase in the tiny-scale
AP50, rising from 0.24 to 0.30. The experimental results
demonstrate consistent improvements in detection perfor-
mance, particularly for small and tiny pedestrians, with-
out significant computational overhead. Ablation studies
further confirm that early-resolution detection is crucial in
enhancing recall for small objects in UAV imagery. These
findings indicate that revisiting the starting level of feature
pyramids is a straightforward yet effective strategy for im-
proving small-object detection in aerial scenarios.
1. Introduction
Unmanned aerial vehicles (UA Vs) are increasingly utilised
in various applications, including traffic monitoring, pub-
lic safety, search and rescue, and crowd analysis [1, 13].
A critical aspect of these applications is the reliable de-
tection of pedestrians from aerial imagery [16]. However,
detecting pedestrians in UA V scenarios poses several chal-
lenges, including extreme scale variations, dense scenes,
complex backgrounds, and the presence of large numbers
of small objects occupying only a few pixels [22]. Re-
cent advancements in small object detection have focused
on improving multi-scale feature fusion, enhancing atten-
tion mechanisms, and designing better localization losses.
While these methods show improvements on general bench-
marks, they often struggle with aerial pedestrian detec-
tion. Pedestrians captured from UA V platforms frequently
appear at very small scales, often below the effective re-
ceptive field of standard detection heads. Several recent
studies have specifically tackled pedestrian detection in
UA V imagery by introducing feature enhancement mod-
ules, multi-scale attention mechanisms, or context-aware
designs. These approaches have shown improved robust-
ness against occlusion and complex backgrounds. How-
ever, most existing work employs conventional detection
heads that start at P3 (1/8 resolution), thereby overlooking
fine-grained spatial information essential for detecting tiny
pedestrians. Lightweight and real-time UA V detectors aim
to reduce computational costs through techniques such as
backbone compression, simplified detection heads, or prun-
ing strategies. Unfortunately, these reductions can lead to
decreased performance when identifying extremely small
objects. Transformer-based methods, including Swin trans-
former approaches for aerial detection [3], improve global
context modeling through hierarchical attention. However,
the use of transformers often increases computational over-
head and does not specifically address the loss of early
spatial resolution for detecting tiny pedestrians. To tackle
these challenges, we propose a high-resolution P2 detec-
tion branch integrated into the YOLOv12 framework. By

2. Related Work
2.1. Small Object Detection in UA V Imagery
Recent years have seen growing interest in small-object de-
tection in aerial imagery. Many studies focus on improv-
ing feature representation through techniques like edge en-
hancement and context modeling. Early research used cas-
cade architectures with deformable convolutions to enhance
the detection of small objects [27]. The work [20] high-
lights the importance of specialised datasets such as AI-
TOD, which reveal the limitations of traditional detectors
for tiny objects and suggest targeted learning strategies. For
instance, CGD-YOLO [2] introduces global edge propa-
gation and multi-dimensional attention mechanisms to im-
prove the extraction of fine contours. EMSANet [8] fur-
ther develops edge-enhanced multi-scale alignment to ad-
dress semantic inconsistencies across different feature lev-
els. Other approaches involve modifications to the back-
bone and feature pyramid designs. UA VDet [25] incorpo-
rates CNN-Mamba hybrid modules and redesigns the pyra-
mid structure specifically for the perception of tiny ob-
jects. WCDB-YOLO [12] employs a dual-backbone strat-
egy combined with wavelet-based context modeling and ex-
plicitly integrates P2 features into the detection head. Ad-
vancements such as High-Resolution Feature Pyramid Net-
works [4] improve multi-scale feature representation, while
QueryDet [23] enhances efficiency by selectively applying
high-resolution computation. Lightweight architectures and
attention-based modules, like the NOV A [15] module in
the RealDroneVision framework, further improve YOLO-
based detection Collectively, these studies emphasize the
importance of maintaining high-resolution spatial informa-
tion when dealing with extremely small targets. However,
accurately detecting extremely small pedestrians in aerial
imagery remains challenging due to limited spatial infor-
mation, underscoring the need for improved high-resolution
detection heads for localising tiny objects.
2.2. UA V Pedestrian detection
Pedestrian detection from UA V platforms has been stud-
ied as a specialized sub-problem due to its critical impor-
tance for safety. Recent works have introduced techniques
such as scale-sensitive feature enhancement [14], attention-
guided fusion [9], and context-aware modules [16] specifi-
cally designed for aerial pedestrian detection. These meth-
ods aim to improve robustness in scenarios involving oc-
clusion and dense crowds. Most UA V detectors focused on
pedestrian detection employ a conventional P3-P5 detection
head structure. However, this structure does not directly
supervise the earliest high-resolution feature maps for de-
tection, which limits the ability to accurately localize ex-
tremely small pedestrians.
Lightweight and real-time UA V detectors [7, 24] have
been developed to reduce computational complexity while
achieving high inference speeds. Unfortunately, they of-
ten struggle to maintain strong performance on tiny ob-
jects due to reduced feature capacity and limitations in
high-resolution modeling. Transformer-based detectors [3]
have shown effectiveness in capturing global relationships,
but they also introduce significant computational over-
head. Recent approaches address this issue by incorporat-
ing attention-based detection heads. For example, EHDC-
YOLO [5] employs a Dynamic Head (DyHead) with multi-
dimensional attention to improve adaptability across vary-
ing object scales. FDE-YOLO [10] integrates scale-aware,
spatial-aware, and task-aware refinements in its detection
head to enhance discrimination in dense scenes. CMA-Net
[24] proposes collaborative multi-attention structures for ef-
ficient real-time UA V detection. Although attention mech-
anisms improve feature weighting, many methods still rely
on the conventional P3–P5 detection hierarchy, which lim-
its sensitivity to ultra-small objects. Recent studies have fo-
cused on attention mechanisms and transformer-based ap-
proaches to improve pedestrian detection across scales. For
example, CoSTAA [18] uses a convolutional Swin Trans-
former with channel and spatial attention to enhance feature
interaction in complex environments. Similarly, MECSA
[19] incorporates a multi-scale attention module for better
feature representation. While these methods improve detec-
tion performance across a range of object sizes, they often
struggle with extremely small pedestrians in aerial imagery
because of insufficient high-resolution spatial features.

have introduced high-resolution branches. WCDB-YOLO
[12] and DPN-YOLO [11] incorporate P2-level features
to strengthen small-object localization. MSAD-YOLO [6]
reconstructs a shallow detection head to preserve spatial
