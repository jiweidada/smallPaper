Abstract 
The performance of deep learning-based object detection methods is heavily dependent 
on the characteristics of training data. Ho wever, real-world detection datasets often 
exhibit severe imbalance in object-scale distributions, resulting in insuﬃcient supervision 
for object sizes with limited instances. To address this issue, we propose a scale-aware 
mosaic augmentation algorithm and a generalized scale-adaptive intersection over union 
(GSIoU)-based varifocal loss (VFL) function. The proposed scale-aware mosaic 
augmentation method alleviates object-scale  imbalance by explicitly regulating scale 
distributions during training sample co nstruction, overcoming the tendency of 
conventional mosaic augmentation to preserve inherent dataset imbalance. Furthermore, 
to handle the limitation of existing IoU-based quality targets that impose relatively large 
penalties for small objects, we replace the lo calization quality target in VFL with GSIoU, 
thereby enabling more consistent classi ﬁcation performance across object scales. We 
evaluate the e ﬀectiveness of the proposed method by  applying the proposed method to 
RT-DETRv2 and conducting experiments on the HRSC2016-MS dataset. Experimental 
results demonstrate that the proposed meth od improves the overall average precision 
(AP) by 1.11AP over the baseline, with a particularly notable improvement of 16.43AP in 
small-object average precision, conﬁrming that the proposed approach achieves balanced 
detection performance across object scales. 
Keywords: object detection; scale imbalance; da ta augmentation; mo saic augmentation; 
varifocal loss; small object detection 
 
1. Introduction 
Object detection is a fundamental task in computer vision and has been widely 
adopted across a variety of real-world applications, including autonomous driving, video 
surveillance, and satellite imagery interpretati on. According to recent  studies (see, e.g., 
[1]), the performance of deep learning-based detectors exhibits a logarithmic relationship 
with respect to the amount of training data, such that increasing the size of training data 
consistently leads to better generalization and overall accuracy. In addition to the amount 
of training data, distributional bias in training data can signi ﬁcantly degrade detection 
performance. Accordingly, data imbalance has been widely studied in object detection,

Objects of diﬀerent sizes present diﬀerent visual attributes and have a direct impact 
on detection performance. Figure 1a illust rates the object-scale distribution of the

Consequently, small objects contribute less to  the training process, receiving fewer and 
weaker supervision signals. A sample image from the HRSC2016-MS dataset is given in

To address scale variation, prior work has explored various strategies, including data 
augmentation techniques such as mosaic [7], crop, and multi-scale training [8], to enhance 
scale diversity. In parallel, architectural modiﬁcations, such as feature pyramid networks 
[9] and multi-resolution feature representations [10], have been proposed to better capture 
objects at di ﬀerent scales. While these approaches have demonstrated e ﬀectiveness in 
improving overall detection performance, they  do not explicitly address the underlying 
imbalance in object size distributions present in real-world datasets. As a result, 
insuﬃciently sampled objects receive weaker supervision during training, and this 
limitation disproportionately aﬀects small objects.

challenging because it occupies only a limited number of pixels relative to the overall 
image resolution. Therefore, small objects are inherently sensitive to annotation noise and 
matching errors during trai ning. Compared to large objects, even minor localization 
inaccuracies can result in substantial relative misalignment for small objects, leading to
