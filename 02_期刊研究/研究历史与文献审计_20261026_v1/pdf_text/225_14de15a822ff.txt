Abstract
Recent advances in gigapixel-level imaging have brought
High-Resolution Wide shots to the forefront of research.
However, these images present significant challenges: ex-
treme sparsity of foreground, gigapixel-level resolutions
and diverse target counts. This makes traditional close-
up detectors inaccurate and slow as they are overwhelmed
by the background. Although previous research has ex-
plored sparse backbones, their fixed sparsity patterns lack
the adaptability required to handle diverse target numbers.
To address this, we introduce ElasticFormer, a sparse back-
bone that dynamically allocates computational resources
based on foreground proportion. After scoring windows
based on variance, proposed ElasticSelector module will
predict the foreground proportion for top-k selection. The
mechanism guides the model to select target-containing
windows, scaling resources in areas where objects are clus-
tered. We introduce a novel loss function combined with the
3-phase training strategy for ElasticSelector, allowing it to
function properly when bounding box annotations are miss-
ing. A WSOD study is carried on PASCAL VOC 2007 to
evaluate its extensibility. Further, ElasticNet is created to
verify its backbone-agnostic nature. In experiments on the
PANDA gigapixel benchmark, ElasticFormer reduces back-
bone FLOPs by 80% while achieving a significant improve-
ment in AP50 when compared to fixed-ratio sparse methods.

1. Introduction
Object detection is a core task in computer vision that
involves both locating and identifying objects of inter-
est in an image. It typically produces, for each detected
instance, a category label and a spatial extent (usually
an axis-aligned bounding box). Models such as Fast R-
CNN [18] have demonstrated strong performance on MS
COCO [25]. However, with the development of nonde-
structive high-resolution imaging systems, High-Resolution
Wide (HRW) shots detection has drawn increasing attention
[6, 16], which refers to object detection in imagery charac-
terized by extremely high spatial resolution and a wide field
of view, such as images captured by gigapixel-scale drone
[33], satellite [12, 13, 37], or panoramic camera [4].
Firstly, in HRW scenarios, foreground density is much
lower than that in conventional detection images. While
typical datasets have foregrounds covering over 50% of
the image, HRW images often contain less than 15% [22],
with large background regions such as sky or vegetation.
Given such extreme sparsity, cutting-edge detectors, includ-
ing DINO [40], Deformable DETR [43], RMT [17], among
others, still struggle with overwhelming background.
Secondly, limited computational resources pose another
challenge. HRW detection scenarios (e.g., UA V-based in-
spection [14] and medical detection [24]) often require real-
time responsiveness. As resolution scales from millions of
pixels in COCO [25] to billions in PANDA [36], the cost of

Thirdly, HRW scenarios show extreme variation in tar-

ure 1(a). Thus models must remain effective across both
sparse and dense cases. However, existing approaches such
as SaccadeDet [23] and SparseFormer [22] overlook this is-
sue, as they rely on fixed sparsity rates that fail to adapt to
fluctuations in target quantity. Consequently, their effective-
ness is significantly reduced when confronted with highly
variable target distributions, illustrated in Figure 1(b).
Inspired by human cognitive efficiency and recent ad-
vances [1, 7, 19, 32, 41], we propose ElasticFormer, a de-
tection framework that introduces elasticity into backbone
design. Unlike fixed-ratio methods that waste computation
in HRW scenes with highly variable foreground proportion,
ElasticFormer dynamically allocates resources by adap-
tively selecting image patches according to the predicted
foreground proportion. A lightweight module, ElasticSe-
lector, guides patch selection independently at each stage
in backbone, enabling fine-grained perception while scal-
ing cost with foreground proportion rather than overall im-
age size. It is noteworthy that ElasticSelector is backbone-
agnostic, motivating the creation of ElasticNet for verifica-
tion. Experiments show that ElasticFormer reduces compu-
tation by up to 80% while improving accuracy compared
with SparseFormer [22], ElasticNet also achieves a 87%
computational saving compared with ResNet-50 [20].
A further challenge in weakly supervised object detec-
tion (WSOD) is the absence of bounding-box annotations,
making foreground proportion hard to compute. To ad-
dress this, we design a new loss function for ElasticSelector,
which uses either ground truth or pseudo labels as supervi-
sion. Validation on the PASCAL VOC 2007 dataset [15]
demonstrates that this extension reduces FLOPs by at least
50% while improving both mAP and CorLoc.
To summarize, our key contributions are:
•We propose ElasticFormer, a novel sparse Vision
Transformer-based backbone that not only addresses the
extremely sparse target distribution and redundant back-
ground in HRW images, but also dynamically adapts to
varying numbers of targets across diverse scenes.
•We further introduce a lightweight component, ElasticS-
elector, which dynamically predicts the foreground pro-
portion during the forward inference process based on
intermediate features, providing efficient guidance with
negligible additional computational overhead.
•We design a label-free loss function that minimizes the
gap between the predicted and actual foreground propor-
tions, enabling self-supervised optimization of ElasticS-
elector without the need for extra annotations.
•We validate the proposed backbone on PANDA [36] and
PASCAL VOC 2007 [15] datasets, and further extend our
backbone-agnostic design to CNN-based architectures,
consistently demonstrating its effectiveness and general-
ity across model families.
2. Related Work
Object Detection in High-Resolution Wide Shots.HRW
detection addresses the critical challenge of locating sparse
objects in gigapixel-scale imagery, where target coverage
is often below 15% and background is highly diverse [36],
different from ImageNet [11]. While general-purpose de-
tectors like Faster R-CNN [18] and Deformable Convolu-
tional Networks [9] achieve strong performance on dense
scenes, their cost scales prohibitively with input resolution,
causing severe latency. Shufflenet [42] reduces complexity
via grouped convolutions, yet lacks adaptive computation.
DynamicDet [27] routes images by predicted difficulty, but
its binary partitioning is ill-suited to HRW shots and main-
taining dual branches is cumbersome. While DGE [31] has
been applied to enhance vision transformer, it lacks the flex-
ibility required to effectively handle HRW scenarios.
Vision Transformers for Detection.Transformers have
revolutionized NLP, and Vision Transformers (ViTs) ex-
tend this success to detection by modeling long-range de-
pendencies. However, conventional ViTs process all image
tokens equally, incurring quadratic complexity with input
size. To reduce cost, methods such as window-based atten-
tion [28, 44], multi-layer self-attention [35], and low-rank
projection [2] have been proposed. Yet these approaches
still face a trade-off between efficiency and accuracy, limit-
ing their effectiveness in HRW detection.
