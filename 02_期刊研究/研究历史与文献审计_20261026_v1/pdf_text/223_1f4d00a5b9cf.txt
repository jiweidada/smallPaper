DM-EFS: Dynamically Multiplexed Expanded Features Set
Form for Robust and Efficient Small Object Detection
Aashish Sharma
KLASS Engineering and Solutions, Singapore
sharma.aashish@klasses.com.sg
Abstract
In this paper, we address the problem of small object detec-
tion (SOD) by introducing our novel approach - Dynami-
cally Multiplexed Expanded Features Set (DM-EFS) form.
Detecting small objects is challenging as they usually suffer
from inadequate feature representation. Hence, to address
this, we propose the Expanded Features Set (EFS) form - a
simple yet effective idea to improve the feature representa-
tion of small objects by utilizing the untapped higher res-
olution features from the shallower layers of the backbone
module. We observe that the EFS form improves the SOD
performance. However, due to processing of additional fea-
tures, it has a higher computational cost which reduces in-
ference efficiency. Hence, to address this, we propose Dy-
namic Feature Multiplexing (DFM) - a novel design that
optimizes the usage of the EFS form during inference by dy-
namically multiplexing it to create our aforementioned DM-
EFS form. Since our DM-EFS form is a multiplexed (or sub-
sampled) optimal version of the EFS form, it improves the
SOD performance like the EFS form but with a lower com-
putational cost. Extensive experiments confirm the efficacy
of our DM-EFS approach. Integrated with YOLOv7 base
model, our DM-EFS achieves state-of-the art results on di-
verse SOD datasets outperforming the base model and SOD
baselines, with on-par or even better inference efficiency.

numerous methods [21, 22, 28, 35, 38] that have achieved
significant performance. Despite this, when used for small
object detection (SOD), e.g., for objects of size ≤ 20px,
these methods do not work well. While there are several
methods [13, 23, 40, 41, 44] specifically designed to ad-
dress the SOD problem, it remains far from solved espe-
cially when working with limited resolution images 1. This

address these issues, we propose our novel approach termed
DM-EFS form which integrates our two main ideas: (1) Dy-

In our investigations, we observe a simple yet effective
way to improve the feature representation of small objects
for limited resolution images; and, that is to utilize the un-
tapped higher resolution features from the shallower layers
of the backbone module. This extension of features in the
backbone − and, subsequently in the neck and head mod-
ules − is what we collectively refer to as the EFS form.
Please refer to Fig. 2a that illustrates the proposed EFS form
and the base (or original) form of a general detection model.
Tested on VisDrone [46] and SODA-D [6] SOD datasets
with YOLOv7 [35] as the base model, SOD results mea-
sured by AP 50 metric show significant improvements with
the EFS form as shown in Fig. 2b. However, due to the in-
creased features processing in the EFS form, it also has a
higher computational cost which reduces the inference fps
efficiency as shown in Fig. 2c; and, to address this issue, we
propose our novel DFM design.
The core idea behind our DFM is to utilize the EFS
form optimally during inference. This is carried out by
dynamically multiplexing the EFS form to obtain the DM-
EFS form with the help of feature multiplexers and their
guiding control module. Hence, our control module is the
module essentially responsible for obtaining the DM-EFS
form. During training, our control module learns (1) a size-
features codebook where each entry of the codebook indi-
cates the smallest subsample of the EFS form that can be
used to reliably detect an object of a given size; and (2)
min and max object sizes present in an image. Then dur-
ing inference, using the learnt size-features codebook, our
control module guides the feature multiplexers to create the
DM-EFS form, which is basically the smallest subsample of
the EFS form that can be used to reliably detect the entire
range of objects in an input image, the range being defined
by its predicted min and max object sizes. This makes our
DM-EFS form provide an improved SOD performance like
the EFS form but with a lower computational cost. Fig. 1d
shows that our DM-EFS approach used with YOLOv7 [35]
base model 2 generates more robust and accurate SOD re-
sults compared to the base model shown in Fig. 1c as well
as other recent SOD baselines shown in Figs. 1a and 1b. In
sum, in this paper, our main contributions are:
1. We propose DM-EFS form - our novel approach for ro-
bust and efficient small object detection (SOD).
2. Our DM-EFS approach integrates our two ideas: (1) Ex-
panded Features Set (EFS) form - a simple yet effec-
tive way to improve the SOD performance by utilizing
the untapped higher resolution features from the back-
bone’s shallower layers; (2) Dynamic Feature Multiplex-
ing (DFM) - our novel design to dynamically multiplex
the EFS form to create our DM-EFS form. Our DM-EFS
form is a multiplexed (or subsampled) optimal version
of the EFS form that provides a similarly improved SOD
performance but with a lower computational cost.
3. Our experiments show that our DM-EFS combined with
YOLOv7 base model achieves state-of-the-art results on
diverse SOD datasets while keeping inference efficiency.
2. Related Works
Object detection is a widely researched area. Existing meth-
ods for general detection are primarily either anchor-free
or anchor-based designs. The anchor-free designs include
FSAF [45], FCOS [34], and GFL [19]. The anchor-based
designs are either multi-stage such as Fast-RCNN [10],
Faster-RCNN [28], FPN [21], and Cascade-RCNN [3]; or
single-stage such as SSD [24, 43], RetinaNet [22], and vari-
ants of YOLO [27] series such as YOLOv7 [35].
Despite the advancements in general detection, small ob-
ject detection (SOD) remains a challenging and relatively
unsolved research area. Earlier SOD works are developed
on techniques such as over-sampling and data augmenta-
2Note, while we use YOLOv7 base model owing to its general robust-
ness, as shown in our experiments later, our proposed DM-EFS approach
is generalizable and also works well with other base models.
