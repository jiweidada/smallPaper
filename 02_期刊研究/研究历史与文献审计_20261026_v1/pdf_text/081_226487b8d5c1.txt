Abstract
Drones are increasingly used in civilian and defense do-
mains, but reliable detection remains challenging due to
their small size, fast motion, and diverse environments. Ex-
isting datasets, such as synthetic benchmarks, fail to cap-
ture real-world variability. We introduce RealDroneVi-
sion, a unified contribution that advances both dataset
and methodology. First, we curate a large-scale real-
world drone detection dataset comprising 173,023 im-
ages, constructed via a semi-automatic pipeline inspired
by self-annotated labeling from videos, enhanced with a
human-in-the-loop to iteratively reduce false positives and
false negatives. This approach yields high-quality an-
notations with reduced manual effort. Second, we pro-
pose the Nano Object Vision Attention (NOVA) module, a
drop-in replacement for YOLOv8’s C2f block. By com-
bining depthwise separable convolutions, scale-aware di-
lated branches, lightweight mixing, and coordinate-aware
attention, our design improves small-object detection while
remaining computationally efficient. Extensive bench-
marks against YOLOv8m/l and YOLOv9c/e demonstrate
that YOLOv8-NOVA dominates across precision (0.912), re-
call (0.870), and mAP@50 (0.920) while being significantly
more lightweight (2.3M params, 5 MB weights). These re-
sults establish RealDroneVision as a strong foundation for
advancing real-world drone detection research.
1. Introduction
Drones have rapidly emerged in civilian, commercial, and
defense applications, ranging from aerial photography and
package delivery to surveillance and public safety monitor-
ing. Alongside these benefits, the misuse of drones poses
serious concerns for security, privacy, and airspace safety.
Consequently, robust drone detection systems are essential
for enabling effective countermeasures and ensuring safe in-
tegration of drones into society.
Despite advances in general-purpose object detection,
drone detection remains particularly challenging. Drones
are typically small, fast-moving, and visually similar to
background clutter, making them harder to localize than
large, well-defined objects. Detection performance is fur-
ther hindered by variations in altitude, lighting, weather,
and drone type. Addressing these challenges requires not
only representative datasets but also architectural innova-
tions that explicitly focus on small-object detection.
Recent works have attempted to close this gap, most no-
tablySynDroneVision[15], which introduced a large-scale
synthetic dataset for drone detection. While synthetic data
offers scalability, it does not fully capture real-world vari-
ability, and SynDroneVision is limited to dataset contribu-
tion without proposing new architectures. As a result, cur-
rent benchmarks may not reflect real-world performance,
and existing detectors often fail to generalize effectively.
In this paper, we presentRealDroneVision, a dual con-
tribution that advances bothdatasetsandarchitecturesfor
drone detection. Our key contributions are as follows:
•Real-World Dataset with Human-in-the-Loop Anno-
tation:We construct a high-quality drone detection

2. Related Work
2.1. Drone Detection Datasets
The availability of datasets has been central to advanc-
ing drone detection and tracking.DUT Anti-UAV[14] re-
mains one of the most comprehensive benchmarks, span-
ning diverse urban and suburban environments across mul-
tiple Chinese cities. It provides tracks for image detec-
tion, video detection, and tracking, but its heavy reliance
on manual annotations makes it costly to scale. The ex-
tendedAnti-UAVdataset further expands this direction with
over 580K annotated bounding boxes in long video se-
quences, highlighting challenges such as drift and occlu-
sion during real-time tracking. Air-to-air benchmarks, such
asDet-Fly[29] (13K UA V images captured from another
UA V) and the dataset by Walter et al. [21] (automatic an-
notation using UVDAR sensors for relative localization),
shift focus to UA V-on-UA V perspectives. More recent ef-
forts attempt to broaden domain variability:SynDroneVi-
sion[15] provides a large synthetic dataset with diverse con-
ditions, whileDrIFT[9] blends synthetic and real domains
across 14 settings to tackle domain shifts. TheICG-Drone
dataset [23] emphasizes multi-drone pursuit–evasion tasks,
while theMDMTbenchmark [16] introduces occlusion-
aware multi-target tracking from multiple drones. Finally,
motion-intensive datasets such asUZH-FPV Drone Rac-
ing[10] capture aggressive quadrotor maneuvers, provid-
ing unique stress tests for vision-based algorithms. Despite
these advances, persistent challenges include scalable anno-
tation, robustness to domain shifts, and the accurate label-
ing of very small or distant UA Vs. Our dataset addresses
these issues via a semi-automatic annotation pipeline with
human verification, yielding high-quality labels for small-
object drones in real-world environments.
2.2. Architectures for Small-Object UA V Detection
Alongside dataset efforts, many works propose architectural
modifications to enhance UA V detection. Several extend the
YOLO family.TGC-YOLOv5[28] integrates transformer
encoders, a global attention module, and coordinate atten-
tion to improve robustness against scale variation and clut-
ter.SOD-YOLO[24] modifies the backbone with a P2 de-
tection layer and attentional fusion modules, demonstrating
improved performance on UA V imagery.Universal Small-
Object YOLO[11] introduces a universal structural design
across YOLO versions, whileImproved YOLOv8[12] bal-
ances small-object accuracy with deployment efficiency.
Beyond UA V-specific works,YOLO-World[7] generalizes
detection to an open-vocabulary setting, providing insights
into robust recognition for small and rare objects. Other
strategies include multi-stage refinement such as theCas-
caded Zoom-In Detector[17], which leverages cropping
and progressive refinement for aerial images. Together,
these efforts highlight the importance of architectural in-
novations for UA V detection, but few combine lightweight
design with explicit optimization for small objects as in our
proposedNOVAblock.
2.3. Applications, Tracking, and System-Level
Works
Drone detection is increasingly integrated into broader anti-
UA V or multi-drone systems. Zhao et al. [27] developed a
vision-based Anti-UA V framework that jointly detects and
tracks drones in intelligent transportation contexts. Xiao
et al. [23] combined the ICG-Drone dataset with Drone-
YOLOSORT, a real-time multi-drone pursuit–evasion sys-
tem optimized for edge deployment. Bari ˇsi´c et al. [5] used
stereo cameras with YOLOv3-Tiny to reconstruct UA V tra-
jectories for interception, validated in field experiments in-
spired by MBZIRC challenges.
