Abstract
Pan-sharpening technology refers to generating a high-
resolution (HR) multi-spectral (MS) image with broad ap-
plications by fusing a low-resolution (LR) MS image and
HR panchromatic (PAN) image. While deep learning
approaches have shown impressive performance in pan-
sharpening, they generally require extensive hardware with
high memory and computational power, limiting their de-
ployment on resource-constrained satellites. In this study,
we investigate the use of binary neural networks (BNNs)
for pan-sharpening and observe that binarization leads to
distinct information degradation across different frequency
components of an image. Building on this insight, we pro-
pose a novel binary pan-sharpening network, termed BN-
NPan, structured around the Prior-Integrated Binary Fre-
quency (PIBF) module that features three key ingredients:
Binary Wavelet Transform Convolution, Latent Diffusion
Prior Compensation, and Channel-wise Distribution Cal-
ibration. Specifically, the first decomposes input features
into distinct frequency components using Wavelet Trans-
form, then applies a “divide-and-conquer” strategy to opti-
mize binary feature learning for each component, informed
by the corresponding full-precision residual statistics. The
second integrates a latent diffusion prior to compensate
for compromised information during binarization, while the
third performs channel-wise calibration to further refine
feature representation. Our BNNPan, developed with the
proposed techniques, achieves promising pan-sharpening
performance on multiple remote sensing datasets, surpass-
ing state-of-the-art binarization algorithms.

Our motivation. Although state-of-the-art DL-based ap-
proaches deliver impressive pan-sharpening performance,
they typically require substantial computational resources,
such as high-end GPUs, rendering them impractical for re-
mote sensing satellites with limited processing and stor-
age capacities. To address this challenge, we investi-
gate network binarization as a potential solution for pan-
sharpening, aiming to reduce computational demands while
preserving performance levels for deployment feasibility.
We first analyze the variations in real-valued activations
after the binarization process. As shown in Fig. 1, the
binarized features encounter significant information loss
compared to the full-precision features. Given the pixel-
level precision required for pan-sharpening, these losses
normally result in substantial performance degradation.
Herein, we address this challenging issue from a fundamen-
tal image processing perspective. Specifically, we investi-
gate the effects of binarization on different frequency com-
ponents, recognizing that each component provides unique
content information. As illustrated in Fig. 1, our analysis re-
veals that: 1) different frequency components respond dif-
ferently to binarization; and 2) applying binarization at the
frequency level preserves a greater amount of feature infor-
mation. This insight motivates the adoption of frequency-
wise binary feature learning for pan-sharpening.
In addition, to address the inherent distributional dispar-
ity between binarized and full-precision features, we de-
velop a full-precision statistics normalized binary convolu-
tion (BConv) unit. As shown in Fig. 2, unlike the conven-
tional BConv unit, our design introduces a lightweight nor-
malization step that leverages full-precision residual statis-
tics to redistribute the binary output, effectively narrowing
the value gap with minimal computational overhead. The
t-SNE visualization in Fig. 2 further demonstrates that our
normalization approach tightly clusters the binary activa-
tions with their full-precision counterparts, significantly en-
hancing representational capability. Building on this, we
propose an effective Binary Wavelet Transform Convolu-
tion (BWTC) block that allows different frequency com-
ponents to attain optimal feature learning, tailored to their
specific characteristics and full-precision statistics. Fol-
lowing BWTC, we further integrate a latent diffusion prior
to compensate for the information lost during binarization.
Lastly, channel-wise attention is introduced to refine the en-
riched feature representations. With these core innovations,
BNNPan achieves competitive performance relative to full-
precision and binary baselines across multiple datasets.
Our contributions are summarized as follows:
• We propose BNNPan, a streamlined and efficient binary
network tailored for remote sensing multi-spectral image
fusion, delivering promising results with lower computa-
tional requirements. To the best of our knowledge, this is
the first attempt to introduce a binary neural network for
pan-sharpening learning.
• We devise a novel binary convolution unit by introducing
a computation-negligible normalization subsequent to its
vanilla counterpart, which utilizes full-precision residual
statistics to redistribute the output of binary convolution,
effectively narrowing the value gap.
• We introduce a latent diffusion module (LDM) to gener-
ate comprehensive and compact image feature represen-
tations, functioning as a degradation-free prior to com-
pensating for the information lost during the binarization
process through a three-stage training strategy.
• Experiments on multiple remote sensing benchmark
datasets show that the proposed BNNPan outperforms
state-of-the-art binary neural networks.
