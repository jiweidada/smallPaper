 b and Yukinobu Taniguchi

Keywords: Vehicle Detection, Downsampling, Convolution, Wavelet Transform.
Abstract: Vehicle detection faces significant challenges due to severe occlusion and scale variation, especially in im-
ages captured by Unmanned Aerial Vehicle (UA V) and Closed-Circuit Television (CCTV). To address these
issues, we propose a novel Separation Strategy that rethinks downsampling operations in object detectors.
Our approach introduces two specialized downsampling modules: Phase-Split Downsampling (PSD) for spa-
tial separation during feature extraction, preserving fine-grained details essential for small vehicle detection;
and Wavelet Transform Downsampling (WTD) for frequency separation during feature fusion, allowing dis-
tinct processing of global context and local details. By integrating these modules into a one-stage detection
framework, we enhance the model’s ability to handle occlusion and scale variation effectively. Extensive ex-
periments on UA V and CCTV datasets demonstrate that our method outperforms state-of-the-art detectors in
both accuracy and efficiency. We improve mAP by up to+2-3% over baseline on VisDrone dataset and+6%
on MLITcctv dataset. This results validate the effectiveness of our Separation Strategy in challenging vehicle
detection scenarios.
1 INTRODUCTION
Object detection technology has developed rapidly in
recent years, leading to the development of several
strong baselines, such as Faster R-CNN (Ren, Shao-
qing , et al., 2015), YOLO (Redmon, Joseph , et al.,
2016), and DETR (Carion, Nicolas , et al., 2020).
These detectors strive to enhance detection accuracy
while making model lightweight, thereby seeing in-
creasing application in fields like industry (Wang,
Dadong , et al., 2021), agriculture (Yamamoto, Yota
, et al., 2025), and transportation (Guo, Yupei , et al.,
2025). Among these applications, object detection
holds particular significance for intelligent transporta-
tion systems. Unlike traditional methods such as radar
(Petrovskaya, Anna , et al., 2009) or Light Detection
And Ranging (LiDAR) (Cao, Xianbin , et al., 2011),
it allows for the precise classification of vehicle type,
a capability that is fundamental to sophisticated traffic
management.

is predominantly performed using images from two
main sources: Unmanned Aerial Vehicle (UA V) (Sri-

As shown in Figure 1b, compared to general object

To address these challenges, some methods focus
on creating hybrid modules that combine convolution
and attention. However, the use of attention mech-
anisms, inevitably increases computational complex-
ity. Furthermore, conventional downsampling opera-
tions in detectors often entangle spatial and frequency
information, leading to the loss of small-scale fea-
tures. This motivates our design of a novel separation
strategy that revisits downsampling as a phase- and
frequency-aware process. Our core premise is that the
optimal downsampling method should adapt to its ar-
chitectural context. The contributions of our work can
be summarized as follows:
i) We propose a novel, dual-paradigm downsam-
pling strategy that applies Phase-Split Downsam-
pling during the feature extraction stage to pre-
serve fine-grained details, and Wavelet Transform
Downsampling during the feature fusion stage to
process global and local information as distinctly
separate entities.
ii) We construct a novel object detector based on this
strategy, designed to handle the diverse and chal-
lenging difficulties posed by severe occlusion and
scale variation in vehicle detection.
iii) We conduct experiments on both UA V and CCTV
datasets. Our proposed model outperforms main-
stream and other state-of-the-art detectors by
achieving the best precision with competitive
computational efficiency. This demonstrates its
effectiveness in improving vehicle detection per-
formance.

2.2 Wavelet-Based Method in
Computer Vision
Wavelet techniques have been increasingly integrated
into computer vision for richer multi-frequency rep-
resentations. Recent works insert wavelet decomposi-
tion into convolutions or Transformer pipelines to en-
hance classification, super-resolution, and detection
performance (Finder, Shahaf E , et al., 2024; Li, Wen-
jie , et al., 2024; Yan, Feng , et al., 2025; Atamna,
Mehdi , et al., 2024). However, most prior studies
place wavelet operations at intermediate layers. In
contrast, our work uses frequency separation explic-
itly during downsampling, assigning low-frequency
components to attention and high-frequency compo-
nents to convolution for complementary processing.
