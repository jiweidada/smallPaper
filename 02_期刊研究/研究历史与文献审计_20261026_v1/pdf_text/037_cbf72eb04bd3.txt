Remote Sensing Small Object Detection Network Based on
Wavelet-Convolution and Fine-Grained Preservation
Hangyu Li
 and Tiecheng Song *
School of Communications and Information Engineering, Chongqing University of Posts and
Telecommunications, Chongqing 400065, China; 2023210433@stu.cqupt.edu.cn
* Correspondence: songtc@cqupt.edu.cn
Abstract
Small object detection in remote sensing imagery is a fundamental task for visual in-
formation extraction, yet it remains challenging due to extremely small target scales,
complex backgrounds, and the loss of discriminative feature information caused by re-
peated downsampling. To address these issues, this paper proposes a Wavelet-Convolution
and Fine-Grained Preservation Network (WCFPNet) based on YOLOv8n. Specifically, a
Wavelet-Convolution Module (WCM) is introduced into the backbone to decompose feature
maps into low- and high-frequency sub-bands, thereby enhancing structural feature mod-
eling and preserving subtle target details. To compensate for the weakened fine-grained
information after repeated downsampling, an Enhanced Spatial Pyramid Pooling-Fast
(ESPPF) module is embedded at the end of the backbone to strengthen multi-scale contex-
tual aggregation. In addition, an Enhanced Feature Pyramid Network (EFPN) is designed
in the neck to facilitate the propagation of shallow and intermediate fine-grained features
to high-level semantic features through cross-level fusion and the Convolutional Block
Attention Module (CBAM). Experiments on the NWPU VHR-10 dataset show that WCF-
PNet achieves 0.879 mAP@0.5 and 0.515 mAP@0.5:0.95, outperforming YOLOv8n by 1.7
and 2.5 percentage points, respectively. Moreover, the proposed WCFPNet achieves a com-
petitive performance compared with several representative detectors while maintaining
moderate model complexity. These results demonstrate the effectiveness of WCFPNet in
challenging remote sensing scenes characterized by complex backgrounds, dense object
distributions, and weak textures.
Keywords: remote sensing images; small object detection; wavelet transform;
fine-grained perception
1. Introduction
Remote sensing small object detection is a fundamental problem in the field of re-
mote sensing visual perception and has important application value in environmental
monitoring, resource exploration, maritime surveillance, disaster assessment, and military
reconnaissance [1]. With the rapid development of remote sensing platforms, unmanned
aerial vehicle imaging, and deep learning techniques, object detection in remote sens-
ing images has made remarkable progress. Nevertheless, this task is still constrained
by several critical challenges, including large scale variations, dense object distributions,
arbitrary orientations, complex backgrounds, and insufficient cross-scene generalization
capability [2–4]. In particular, in high-resolution and long-range aerial imaging scenarios,
small objects usually occupy only a very limited number of pixels, while weak textures, low

From the perspective of benchmark datasets, general object detection benchmarks
such as COCO [7] have greatly advanced the development of detection frameworks for
natural scenes. In contrast, remote sensing and aerial image benchmarks, such as DOTA [8],
tiny object detection in Aerial Images [ 9], and the tiny object detection benchmark con-
structed based on the Normalized Wasserstein Distance [10], further highlight the unique
characteristics of small objects in remote sensing and aerial imagery, particularly in terms
of scale variation, object density, orientation variations, and background complexity. Com-
pared with natural images, small objects in remote sensing imagery usually exhibit larger
intra-class variations and higher inter-class similarity and are more susceptible to detail
loss and localization deviation during repeated downsampling and feature aggregation. As
a result, existing general-purpose detectors often struggle to maintain stable performance
when directly applied to such detection scenarios. To address these challenges, existing
studies have explored various solutions, including multi-scale feature fusion, attention
mechanisms, dynamic convolution, dilated convolution, Transformer-based global mod-
eling, loss function optimization, and label assignment strategies. These techniques have
been widely adopted in modern detectors, including YOLO-series models, to improve fea-
ture extraction and contextual modeling for remote sensing small object detection [11–14].
In particular, deformable convolution enhances geometric modeling capability through
adaptive sampling and provides important insights for object representation in complex
remote sensing scenes.
However, most existing multi-scale and attention-based detectors primarily enhance
feature representation in the spatial domain through resampling, feature aggregation, or
attention recalibration. Although these strategies improve semantic interaction among dif-
ferent feature levels, such spatial-domain designs do not explicitly decouple low-frequency
structural information from high-frequency edge and texture details before feature fusion.
For small objects in remote sensing imagery, such high-frequency features are usually weak
and easily entangled with background clutter during repeated downsampling. Therefore,
spatial-domain multi-scale fusion alone may be insufficient to preserve discriminative
small-object details.
This reveals a specific research gap: existing methods usually attempt to recover or
enhance features after spatial resolution degradation has occurred, whereas the preser-
vation of discriminative fine-grained information before it is severely weakened remains
insufficiently explored. In particular, for remote sensing small objects with weak textures,
low contrast, and high background similarity, simply increasing multi-scale fusion paths or
attention modules may not fundamentally address the frequency-domain entanglement
between target details and background noise.
To address this gap, we build upon YOLOv8n and propose a Wavelet-Convolution and
Fine-Grained Preservation Network (WCFPNet) for remote sensing small object detection.
Specifically, a Wavelet-Convolution Module (WCM) is introduced into the backbone to
explicitly decompose feature maps into low-frequency structural components and high-
frequency detail components, thereby improving frequency-selective representation under
complex background interference. An Enhanced Spatial Pyramid Pooling-Fast (ESPPF)
module is further employed to enhance deep contextual aggregation while reducing exces-
sive smoothing of local details. In addition, an Enhanced Feature Pyramid Network (EFPN)
is designed to propagate shallow and intermediate fine-grained cues to high-level semantic
features through attention-guided cross-level fusion. In this way, the proposed framework
