Abstract 
Unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV) object detection plays an essential role in modern visual 
perception, but it remains challenging because UAV imagery typically contains extremely 
small, densely distributed objects embedded in complex backgrounds. Conventional de-
tectors, including the recent YOLOv12, are prone to losing critical spatial details during 
downsampling and often exhibit task misalignment between classification and localiza-
tion, particularly under severe scale variations. To address these problems, this study pro-
poses SPTD-YOLO, a small -object-aware pyramidal and task -aligned dynamic detector. 
Specifically, a Small Object Enhanced Pyramid (SOEP) is developed by incorporating 
SPDConv and CSPOmniKernel to preserve and refine shallow , fine-grained features. In 
addition, a high-resolution P2 detection layer is introduced to increase spatial grid density 
and strengthen the structural representation of tiny objects. Furthermore, a Task -Aligned 
Dynamic Detection Head (TADDH) is designed to decouple and coordinate classification 
and regression through dynamic convolution and a synergistic dual -gating mechanism. 
Experiments on VisDrone2019 show that SPTD -YOLO improves mAP@0.5 by 8.37% and 
mAP@0.5:0.95 by 5.11% over YOLOv12 while maintaining practical efficiency for UAV 
edge deployment. 
Keywords: UAV object detection; YOLOv12; small object detection; feature enhancement; 
dynamic head; task alignment 
 
1. Introduction 
Object detection is a fundamental task in computer vision and underpins a wide 
range of real -world applications, including intelligent transportation, public safety, dis-
aster response, and environmental monitoring [1,2]. These applications require visual per-
ception systems that can reliably localize and recognize targets in dynamic and unpre-
dictable environments [3]. Although ground-based monitoring systems have been exten-
sively developed, modern surveillance scenarios increasingly demand greater spatial flex-
ibility and broader coverage than fixed cameras can provide [4]. In this context, un-
manned aerial vehicles (UAVs) have become indispensable sensing platforms, as they 
overcome the physical limitations of stationary sensors by offering high spatial mobility

However, the shift from ground-level imaging to aerial perspectives introduces sub-
stantially more complex visual challenges [6]. In critical scenarios such as large-scale event 
security and urban emergency response, rapid -response UAVs are frequently deployed 
to monitor crowd dynamics, track scattered vehicles, and assist ground rescue operations. 
During mass public gatherings or emergency evacuations, these missions require UAVs 
to continuously detect highly dense and overlapping targets from high -altitude view-
points. As a result, small target instances, such as pedestrians and vehicles, often appear 
at long distances and occupy only a few pixels, sometimes less than 32 × 32 pixels, making 
them highly susceptible to being obscured by heterogeneous background clutter [7,8]. 
The VisDrone2019 dataset is a representative benchmark that captures these chal-
lenging aerial imaging conditions [6]. Collected from diverse urban scenes, it contains 
high-resolution images in which targets are densely distributed, affected by frequent 
viewpoint changes, and characterized by substantial scale variations. Representative ex-
amples illustrating these aerial  detection challenges are shown in Figure 1. In addition, 
the dataset covers ten categories from daily scenarios and exhibits a pronounced class 
imbalance, with a distribution strongly biased toward densely arranged small instances 
[9]. Taken together, these coupled challenges, including extreme scale compression, spa-
tial crowding, and category imbalance, severely test the feature representation capacity of 
conventional detection architectures. This difficulty is further amplified by the stringent 
real-time inference and edge -deployment requirements of onboard UAV platforms 
[10,11].
