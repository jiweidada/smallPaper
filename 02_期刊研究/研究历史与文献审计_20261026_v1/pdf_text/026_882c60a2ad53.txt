Abstract
Precise 3D medical image segmentation is a clinical cor-
nerstone for diagnosis, therapy planning, and longitudinal
monitoring. However, routine acquisition with anisotropic
voxel spacing and heterogeneous reconstruction induces
downsampling aliasing and cross-scale misalignment that
blur boundaries, fragment topology, and undermine reli-
ability. Existing U-shaped CNN or Transformer designs
neither control alias injection at decimation nor explic-
itly align high-resolution evidence before decoder fusion,
leading to unstable interfaces under device and protocol
variability. We introduce the Coset-fibRated micrO-local
co-attention Network (CROWn), a general segmentation
framework that couples sampling theory with represen-
tation learning to jointly suppress aliasing and calibrate
cross-scale fusion. CROWn comprises two complemen-
tary components. The Microlocal Polyphase Co-Attentive
Decimator (µPCAD) performs axis-aware polyphase anal-
ysis with pooled–subband co-attention and explicit anti-
alias low-pass, routing boundary-relevant high-frequency
evidence while attenuating spurious phase components dur-
ing downsampling. The Octaphase Coset Fibration (OCF)
anti-aliases high-resolution skips, restructures them via
3D space-to-depth into cosets, and applies phase atten-
tion with edge-gated modulation to deliver compact, phase-
aligned, boundary-aware features to the decoder. Exten-
sive evaluations across 15 publicly available datasets span-
ning CT, MRI, and OCT demonstrate CROWn’s state-of-
the-art performance against 17 recent leading methods, im-
proves overlap and topological consistency, consistently re-
duces boundary errors, while maintaining controlled train-

1. Introduction
Reliable volumetric delineation is a prerequisite for quanti-
tative imaging workflows such as tumor volumetry, organ-
at-risk contouring, and longitudinal disease burden track-
ing in CT, MRI, and OCT [18]. However, routine acquisi-
tion and multi-site aggregation introduce physical and pro-
tocol variability that directly perturbs the statistics of the
input volume and the stability of downstream segmentors
[9]. Anisotropic voxel spacing and modality incur partial-
volume effects and slice-thickness aliasing. Furthermore,
heterogeneous reconstruction kernels, denoising pipelines,
and intensity scaling shift both spatial statistics and fre-
quency occupancy [58]. These factors degrade edge gra-
dients, distort boundaries, and produce plane-to-plane in-
consistencies that standard isotropic kernels and patch tok-
enization do not explicitly correct [58]. As a result, mod-
els trained in a single regime often entangle device-specific
cues with anatomy to limit cross-domain generalization [9].
A formulation that treats sampling, aliasing, and boundary
evidence as coupled problems, becomes necessary for trust-
worthy 3D segmentation under realistic acquisition.
Despite rapid progress from ConvNets to volumetric
Transformers, three mechanism-level gaps persist [12].
(i) Downsampling aliasing and edge attenuation. Strided
convolutions and pooling compress spectra without anti-
aliasing to suppress thin structures and amplify staircase ar-
tifacts; spacing-agnostic isotropic filters misalign tri-axial
evidence under anisotropy [58]. (ii) Scale and pathway
misalignment. Decoders often fuse high-resolution skips
that propagate aliased high-frequency components, while

low-resolution semantics lack boundary-aware inductive
bias. Moreover, skip concatenation yields poorly calibrated
multi-scale integration [63]. (iii) Scanner and protocol in-
duced domain shift. For example, learned features fre-

ate noise at the expense of clinically salient edges [3]. These
issues motivate a design that jointly controls aliasing dur-
ing decimation and structures the fusion of high-resolution
evidence so that boundary localization and structural coher-
ence are preserved across sampling regimes and devices.
Hence, we introduce Coset-fibRated micrO-local co-
attention Network (CROWn), a framework that inte-
grates sampling theory with representation learning via
two complementary components. Microlocal Polyphase
Co-Attentive Decimator (µPCAD) performs axis-aware
polyphase analysis and co-attention during downsampling.
It extracts local extrema and background context, decom-
poses features into multi-orientation subbands, and com-
putes cross-source attention that treats pooled branches as
queries and keys, and wavelet subbands as values. The fu-
sion is recalibrated and then decimated with an explicit anti-
aliasing blur, yielding low-resolution embeddings that re-
tain boundary-relevant high-frequency structure while sup-
pressing spurious components. Octaphase Coset Fibration
(OCF) restructures skip connections before decoder fusion.
High-resolution features are anti-aliased and transformed
via 3D space-to-depth into eight interleaved cosets. Phase
attention then refines these cosets while an edge-gated term
derived from 3D Sobel responses modulates the features
[44, 59]. Furthermore, depthwise-separable aggregation
produces compact, phase-aligned skips that are semanti-
cally closer to the decoder scale and less prone to transmit
aliasing.µPCAD and OCF couple microlocal co-attention
for alias-aware decimation with coset-fibrated, edge-guided
skip calibration, addressing the failure modes of volumetric
segmentation under anisotropy and device heterogeneity.
Evaluations across diverse benchmarks show consis-
tent accuracy and boundary quality under heterogeneous
sampling. CROWn uses 23.78 M Params and 199.58 G
FLOPs. Ablation studies corroborate the effectiveness of
theµPCAD and OCF designed, and deploying both mod-
ules across stages yields the most stable boundary localiza-
tion. The main contributions are summarized as follows:
• Proposing CROWn for 3D medical image segmenta-
tion, linking polyphase sampling and microlocal analysis
with multi-scale feature learning to address aliasing and
boundary degradation.
• DesigningµPCAD, a decimator that integrates pooling-
derived queries/keys with wavelet subband values to
perform alias-aware, boundary-preserving downsampling
with explicit anti-aliasing.
• Developing OCF to restructure high-resolution skips
via octaphase coset decomposition, phase attention, and
edge-gated modulation to deliver compact, phase-aligned,
boundary-aware skip features.
• Extensive experiments across 15 benchmarks covering
CT, MRI, and OCT validate the effectiveness ofµPCAD
and OCF, along with model complexity reporting.
2. Related Work
In 3D medical image segmentation, CNNs established the
voxel-wise paradigm, pioneered by 3D U-Net [6] and V-Net
[34] and extended by RAUNet [36], ResUNet [53], Seg-
ResNet [35], MultiResUNet [17], HighRes3DNet [23], and
uC 3DU-Net [16], while nnU-Net [18] provided a strong,
adaptable baseline. Yet pure CNNs are constrained by lo-
cal receptive fields and anisotropic sampling. To address
long-range dependencies, Transformer-based [46] designs
have surged: ViT [8] and Swin Transformer [28] enable
hierarchical self-attention, and hybrid architecture such as
TransUNet [4], TransBTS [47], UNETR [12], SwinUNETR
[11], UNETR++ [43], SegFormer3D [40], UNesT [57],
Slim UNETR [37], D-Former [49], TransHRNet [54], PH-
Trans [26], LW-CTrans [20], WaveFormer [1], MAT3D [45]
and nnWNet [62] surpass classic CNNs on multi-organ and
tumor benchmarks. In parallel, ConvNeXt [29] reexam-
ines convolution, leveraging large kernels and modern nor-
malization and activation, to match or exceed Transformers
with better compute and memory efficiency; ConvNeXt-
based 3D UX-Net [22] and MedNeXt [42] excel on high-
resolution CT and MRI. Most recently, state-space models
(SSM) capture long dependencies with linear complexity:
Mamba [10] and its vision variants power architectures [27]
such as U-Mamba [32], Swin-UMamba [24], SegMamba
[50], and SegMamba v2 [52], improved robustness to small
lesions and slender anatomical structures.
Despite rapid progress, no prevailing approach
jointly delivers global context, fine-grained detail, and
deployment-level efficiency under anisotropic sampling.
Our method addresses this gap and yields consistent gains
on multi-organ and tumor segmentation.
