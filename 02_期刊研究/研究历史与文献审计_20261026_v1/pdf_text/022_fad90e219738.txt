Abstract
Graphite ore grade sorting is a key step toward intelligent mineral processing; however, it
faces three representational contradictions: ambiguous classification posteriors at grade
boundaries, asymmetric multi-scale feature interaction, and the mismatch between class-
agnostic self-distillation assignment and sample-level difficulty. Targeting these, this
paper adopts D-FINE as the baseline and introduces three decoupled improvements at its
decoder, encoder, and criterion layers. (1) Boundary-Grade-aware Distribution Refinement
(BG-FDR) online identifies boundary samples via the Top-2 classification score gap and
modulates regression-distribution refinement, yielding +2.69 percentage points in mAP@0.5
with zero additional trainable parameters. (2) Bidirectional Feature Pyramid with Global–
Local Spatial Attention (BiFPN-GLSA) builds a learnable weighted bidirectional multi-
scale fusion path. (3) Difficulty-Aware Decoupled Distillation with Wise-Inner-Shape-IoU
(DADD+Wise-IoU) imposes class- and sample-level difficulty-aware constraints. In the
integrated full model, this increases Precision from 66.21% to 71.43% (+5.22 pp), F1 from
73.57% to 77.57%, and mean IoU from 97.81% to 98.35%, while false positives drop by
19.6%; the only parameter overhead (+3.84M) comes from BiFPN-GLSA, with BG-FDR
and DADD adding effectively no network weights. Ablation on a self-built 3800-image
dataset reveals a non-monotonic AP–Precision relationship: the mAP-optimal configuration
(BG-FDR+BiFPN-GLSA, 94.17%) and the Precision-optimal one (DADD+Wise-IoU, 77.54%)
do not coincide, providing a quantitative basis for objective-driven module selection in
industrial sorting.
Keywords: graphite ore sorting; object detection; D-FINE; boundary-aware distribution
refinement; bidirectional feature pyramid; difficulty-aware distillation; global–local
spatial attention

sorting accuracy of graphite ore grade (carbon content) directly determines the subsequent
smelting efficiency and the quality of the final product. Traditional graphite ore grade
sorting relies on manual visual inspection, which suffers from inherent drawbacks such as
high labor intensity, strong subjectivity, and low efficiency, making it difficult to meet the
urgent demand for the intelligent upgrading of modern mines.
In recent years, deep learning-driven object detection has opened new technical path-
ways for intelligent mineral sorting. Our research group has conducted systematic studies
on graphite ore grade detection, and its technical route exhibits a clear paradigm evolu-
tion from coarse-grained classification to fine-grained localization. Early work adopted
image-level classification as the mainstream paradigm, where Huang et al. [1] achieved
high-accuracy grade classification based on an improved Inception-ResNet-v2 with LDP-
Conv and SE attention; subsequently, Qiu et al. [2] proposed GS-YOLO-seg to extend the
paradigm to instance-level segmentation, achieving a lightweight design with GSConv
and C3k2-Faster; more recently, GOG-RT-DETR [3] marks our group’s formal shift to the
end-to-end detection paradigm, raising mAP@0.5 to 83.7% through a Faster-Rep-EMA back-
bone and a BiFPN-GLSA feature pyramid; in parallel, DW-YOLO [4] and Stellar-YOLO [5]
improved the YOLO series through multi-branch receptive-field enhancement and extreme
lightweighting, respectively. Furthermore, the group has extended this task-aware design
methodology to general ore detection [6].
However, examining the above evolution reveals that prior work has been highly
concentrated on the feature extraction and fusion stage—whether represented by the Faster-
Rep-EMA backbone replacement and BiFPN-GLSA feature enhancement of GOG-RT-DETR
or by the lightweight backbone designs of Stellar-YOLO and DW-YOLO—with their essence
belonging to the “stronger feature representation” paradigm. The common limitation of
these works lies in insufficient attention to several deep-seated questions—notably, how
the refinement mechanism of the regression distribution in the detection head responds
to sample discriminative uncertainty, and how the distillation signal in the loss function
perceives the inter-class detection difficulty discrepancy. In other words, our work does
not simply replace the baseline framework from RT-DETR with D-FINE—although D-
FINE’s Fine-grained Distribution Refinement (FDR) and GO-LSD mechanism provide the
necessary technical premise for solving the above problems—but rather introduces targeted
methodological adaptations at the two underlying logics of boundary-aware optimization
and difficulty-decoupled distillation.
Starting from the visual representation characteristics of graphite ore images, the
reason the above “feature-representation-centric” paradigm hits a performance ceiling lies
in a fundamental representational conflict between its uniform optimization strategy and
the three types of visual heterogeneity of graphite ore:
Contradiction I: Representational mismatch between ambiguous classification pos-
terior and uniform regression constraints. Graphite ore grades are discretized by carbon
content into three tiers: low (0–10%), medium (10–20%), and high (20%+). In the grade-
boundary neighborhood (low–medium boundary 9–11%, medium–high boundary 18–22%),
the visual representations of samples are highly coupled—texture, color, and particle mor-
phology are nearly indistinguishable, causing the classifier’s output posterior P(y|x) to
approach a uniform distribution. However, both D-FINE’s FDR mechanism [7] and GOG-
RT-DETR’s regression constraints [3] impose distribution refinement iterations of equal
strength on all samples; their implicit assumption—“samples with higher classification
confidence also have higher localization accuracy”—systematically fails in the boundary
neighborhood. This mismatch between posterior ambiguity and optimization uniformity
leads to significantly insufficient convergence of the regression distribution for boundary
samples, constituting the root cause of grade misjudgment.
