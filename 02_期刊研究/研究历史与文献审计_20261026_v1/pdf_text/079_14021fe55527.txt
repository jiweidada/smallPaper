Abstract
Underwater images commonly suffer from foreground-
background ambiguity, loss of structural details, and
severely reduced contrast, which collectively make under-
water object detection (UOD) an inherently challenging
task. To handle this issue, we present a residual-guided
hierarchical calibration network (RHCNet) designed to
achieve more efficient and robust UOD, which comprises a
residual-guided feature enhancement module (RGFE) and
a hierarchical feature calibration pyramid module (HFCP).
Concretely, RHCNet extends the standard ResNet-50 back-
bone by embedding the RGFE, which effectively strengthens
the representation of edge and texture features in blurry re-
gions by jointly leveraging convolutional operations and at-
tention mechanisms to achieve more discriminative feature
extraction for UOD. Subsequently, the HFCP integrates
a bottom-up semantic enhancement path and a top-down
fine-grained feature compensation path, while a K-means
clustering–guided feature calibration module is jointly em-
ployed to ensure multi-level cross-scale semantic consis-
tency and accurate alignment of salient region features. Ex-
tensive experiments on the DUO and UTDAC benchmark
datasets demonstrated that our RHCNet attains the high-
est AP scores of 70.53% and 53.35%, respectively. Be-
sides, our RHCNet also maintains excellent detection ac-
curacy and strong generalization capability on the COCO
dataset for terrestrial scenarios. The code is available at
https://github.com/YitengGuo/RHCNet.

This study shows that underwater object detection per-
formance is mainly constrained by feature confusion and in-
sufficient structural modeling. Specifically, the foreground
object and background are highly similar in color, texture,
and illumination distribution, making it difficult for tradi-
tional detectors to focus on the real object area during train-
ing. For instance, the ROI Pooling feature of FRCNN [7]
includes extensive background information. This semantic
confusion will cause the feature response to be incorrectly
offset, weakening the object discrimination capability. Un-
like ordinary natural scene detection, underwater detection
requires stable semantic focus and cross-scale feature align-
ment in complex interference environments. However, ex-
isting methods often lack explicit mechanisms to separate
foreground and background, resulting in noise accumula-
tion in the feature transfer stage. Comprehensive analysis
indicates that existing detectors face three major challenges:
(1) lack of explicit structural modeling of blurred object
areas; (2) difficulty in achieving effective semantic focus
under severe foreground background interference; and (3)
alignment deviations during multi-scale feature transmis-
sion hinder efficient cross-scale fusion.
Therefore, we present a Residue-guided Hierarchical
Calibration Network (RHCNet) to systematically alleviate
the three significant challenges of ambiguous boundaries,
semantic misalignment, and uneven structure. Unlike pre-
vious detectors that relied on complex structures, RHCNet
has achieved systematic optimization through feature focus-
ing, semantic calibration, and scale alignment via a three-
level collaborative mechanism. Specifically, RHCNet in-
corporates a RGFE into the ResNet backbone to address the
loss of texture structures in blurred regions, which lever-
ages residual information guidance and multi-level feature
interaction strategies to strengthen the perception of edge
contours and texture structures. Secondly, we design K-
means cluster-guided calibration, position-aware, and nor-
malized activation modules in the HFCP to alleviate the
semantic defocusing caused by foreground–background in-
terference, wherein bidirectional information flow is em-
ployed to achieve effective feature concentration and back-
ground suppression. Finally, we further incorporate a cross-
scale calibration mechanism that addresses alignment devi-
ations among multi-scale features by performing context-
guided saliency aggregation, thereby achieving precise in-
tegration of fine-grained structural cues and high-level se-
mantic representations to improve the overall detection ro-
bustness and feature coherence. Thanks to this end-to-end
residual guidance and hierarchical calibration design, RHC-
Net has significantly improved the accuracy and stability
of detection in complex underwater scenarios. As illus-
trated in Fig. 1, our RHCNet accurately focuses on the
object region even in underwater scenarios characterized
by background-object confusion, blurred object boundaries,
and low-light conditions. Satisfactorily, the bounding box
results demonstrate high consistency with the actual object
locations. The visualization further validates the effective-
ness of our proposed RHCNet in achieving precise object
recognition within complex underwater environments.
Specifically, the primary contributions of this study can
be outlined as follows.
1) We propose a residual attention–guided hierarchical
calibration network (RHCNet) that centers on improving
the structural saliency and optimizing the semantic con-
sistency, thereby establishing a comprehensive detection
framework that seamlessly integrates fine-grained struc-
tural perception, discriminative region focusing, and cross-
scale collaborative calibration to achieve robust and accu-
rate UOD.
2) We propose a residual-guided feature enhancement
module (RGFE) that adopts a residual-information-driven
strategy to reinforce texture perception and boundary sen-
sitivity, thereby effectively restoring discriminative features
in underwater scenes where object boundaries are blurred
and structural details are degraded.
3) We propose a hierarchical feature calibration pyramid
module (HFCP) that integrates a hierarchical calibration
strategy with bidirectional semantic flow to facilitate cross-
level information exchange and adaptive feature alignment,
thereby effectively mitigating semantic misalignment in-
duced by foreground-object occlusion.
4) We integrate residual guidance with a multi-path con-
text calibration mechanism to achieve a synergistic en-
hancement of fine-grained representations and high-level
semantics, thereby effectively alleviating alignment bias
and structural misalignment in fuzzy regions during multi-
scale feature transfer in underwater scenes.
2. Related Work
Underwater object detection (UOD). UOD has long been
affected by light attenuation, color distortion, and back-
ground interference, resulting in blurred object boundaries,
reduced contrast, and unstable features. Early research
predominantly employed image enhancement and dehazing
methods to improve visual quality. However, such prepro-
cessing methods often compromise structural consistency
and struggle to improve detection accuracy [4, 6, 23]. With
the advancement of the deep detector Faster RCNN [9],
end-to-end learning methods have been widely adopted for
underwater detection. However, the structural expressive
capability of convolutional features in ambiguous regions
