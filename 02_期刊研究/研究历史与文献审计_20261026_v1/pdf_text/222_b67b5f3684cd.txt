Abstract
Point-Supervised Object Detection (PSOD) in a discrim-
inative style has recently gained significant attention for
its impressive detection performance and cost-effectiveness.
However, accurately predicting high-quality pseudo-box la-
bels for drone-view images, which often feature densely
packed small objects, remains a challenge. This difficulty
arises primarily from the limitation of rigid sampling strate-
gies, which hinder the pseudo-box optimization process.
To address this, we propose PointSR, an effective and ro-
bust point-supervised object detection framework with self-
regularized sampling that integrates temporal and infor-
mative constraints throughout the pseudo-box generation
process. Specifically, the framework comprises three key
components: Temporal-Ensembling Encoder (TE Encoder),
Coarse Pseudo-box Prediction, and Pseudo-box Refine-
ment. The TE Encoder builds an anchor prototype library
by aggregating temporal information for dynamic anchor
adjustment. In Coarse Pseudo-box Prediction, anchors are
refined using the prototype library, and a set of informa-
tive samples is collected for subsequent refinement. During
Pseudo-box Refinement, these informative negative sam-
ples are used to suppress low-confidence candidate posi-
tive samples, thereby improving the quality of the pseudo-
boxes. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demon-
strate that PointSR significantly outperforms state-of-the-
art methods, achieving up to 2.6% ∼ 7.2% higher AP 50
using only point supervision. Additionally, it exhibits strong
robustness to perturbation in human-labeled points.
1. Introduction
Drone-view object detection (DroneDet) [7, 23, 27, 28, 33],
which involves detecting objects from drone-captured im-
ages, plays a vital role in various domains, including na-
tional defense, resource management, and drone inspection.

However, DroneDet models often experience a significant
performance drop when applied to new scenes or datasets
due to their limited generalization capabilities. A straight-
forward solution is to collect and annotate new datasets and
retrain the models. However, annotating drone-view images
with box-level annotations containing accurate position in-
formation is particularly challenging due to the prevalence
of many densely packed small targets, far more complex
than annotating ground-view images. Moreover, the diver-
sity of the scenes and object types can lead to combinato-
rial explosions of labeling requirements, making the process
infeasible in many cases. Consequently, constructing new
datasets with box-level annotations is both time-consuming
and costly when deploying drones in new scenarios.
To alleviate the cost associated with box-level annota-
tions, Image-Supervised Object Detection (ISOD) [1, 14,
26] has been extensively studied. ISOD treats images as a
bag of regions, assuming at least one region contains the ob-
ject and uses a Multiple Instance Learning (MIL) classifier
to identify regions corresponding to image-level labels. De-

spite its labeling efficiency, ISOD methods often perform
poorly in complex scenarios such as drone-view images,
with targets typically small and densely packed, due to the
lack of essential position information in image-level labels.
To overcome this limitation, Point-Supervised Object
Detection (PSOD) has emerged as an alternative solution.
PSOD provides minimal and yet additional distinctive po-
sition information for each object and has gained signifi-
cant attention in tasks such as detection [10, 32], localiza-
tion [30, 31], and segmentation [12, 20]. According to [17],
the labor cost of point annotations per object is only about
1/18 of that for bounding boxes, making PSOD a highly
cost-effective solution for DroneDet applications.
Existing PSOD methods [3, 10, 11, 15, 29, 32] can be
broadly categorized into generative and discriminative ap-
proaches. Generative methods [10, 11, 29] predict a masked
region covering a target labeled by a point and then derive a
pseudo box by estimating the minimum circumscribed rect-
angle of the region. However, pixels in the predicted mask
of each object are susceptible to misclassification, result-
ing in inaccurate and unstable pseudo-box predictions. Dis-
criminative methods [3, 15, 32] first generate a bag of pro-
posal boxes for each label and then select the optimal one
as the final pseudo-box through an MIL-based optimization
process, as illustrated in Fig. 1(a). A reasonable sampling
strategy aiming to extract the labeled training set from an
unlabeled dataset is critical for the successful training of
the MIL classifier. Despite their potential, these methods
still face two significant challenges, as shown in Fig. 2(a):
1) Static positive sampling in an anchor style leads to train-
ing instability, especially in the early stages (see the blue
curves in Fig. 7); 2) Inefficient negative sampling that ran-
domly samples background regions introduces numerous ir-
relevant negative samples, reducing training efficiency.
To address these limitations, we propose PointSR, an ef-
fective and robust point-supervised DroneDet framework
with self-regularized sampling. As shown in Fig. 1(b),
PointSR integrates temporal and informative constraints
throughout the pseudo-box generation process to produce
high-quality pseudo-box labels. Temporal-Ensembling En-
coder (TE Encoder) first builds an anchor prototype library
by collecting and encoding temporal information from
pseudo-box labels during refinement. Then, the Coarse
Pseudo-box Generation adjusts anchors using the anchor
prototype library to produce better coarse pseudo boxes
while collecting a set of informative samples for the sub-
sequent refinement. Finally, during the Refinement stage,
the collected informative samples are used to suppress
low-confidence candidate positive samples to refine pseudo
boxes. Benefiting from this self-regularized sampling strat-
egy, PointSR not only ensures a more stable and efficient
training process but also effectively improves the model’s
robustness to perturbation brought by manual labeling.

Our key contributions can be summarized as follows:
1) We propose PointSR, a novel and robust framework
for point-supervised DroneDet, capable of generating high-
quality pseudo-box labels from point-level supervision.
2) We design a Temporal-Ensemble Encoder for stable
pseudo-box prediction and an informative negative sam-
pling strategy to enhance pseudo-box quality.
3) PointSR achieves an average AP 50 of 42.5%, 38.7%,
and 35.0% on three drone-captured benchmark datasets,
i.e., DroneVehicle [22], UA VDT [5], and VisDrone [34]
with only point supervision, outperforming the previous
best methods by 7.2%, 5.4%, and 2.6%, respectively.
2. Related Work
In this section, we briefly review the current state of re-
search on ISOD, PSOD and PSOD for aerial images.
2.1. Image-Supervised Object Detection (ISOD)
ISOD refers to an approach in which a detector is trained
with image-level annotations rather than box-level ones.
The pioneering work WSDDNs [1] integrated the MIL into
ISOD, employing a dual-stream structure to classify pro-
posals, and enabling effective weakly-supervised object de-
tection. Inspired by this, Tang et al. [24] designed a Pro-
posal Cluster Learning (PCL) approach, which used pro-
posal clusters as supervision to infer rough object locations.
However, image-level annotations lack explicit position in-
formation, leading to low-quality proposals, such as back-
ground regions, in the potential object tool. This limitation
makes it infeasible to directly apply ISOD to DroneDet.

ods [2, 3, 17, 21] published in recent years. By leveraging
