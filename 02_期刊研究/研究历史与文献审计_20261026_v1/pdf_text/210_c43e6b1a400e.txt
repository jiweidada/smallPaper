Abstract
Fisheye cameras are increasingly employed in traffic
surveillance systems due to their ultra-wide field of view,
which allows for extensive scene coverage with a reduced
number of devices. However, object detection in fisheye im-
ages remains challenging for real-time applications. Many
recent methods rely on multi-model ensembles to boost
accuracy, but these approaches incur high computational
costs and latency. Moreover, fisheye distortion causes pe-
ripheral objects to appear small and warped, making them
difficult to detect and leading to unstable model conver-
gence. In this work, we present an efficient and scalable
framework for fisheye object detection that addresses these
challenges from both algorithmic and system perspectives.
We propose a distortion-aware learning strategy that im-
proves detection performance in highly distorted regions
and a spatially guided augmentation method to enhance
robustness. To support real-time deployment, we imple-
ment a set of GPU-level runtime optimizations that signifi-
cantly reduce memory usage and latency. Extensive experi-
ments on benchmark datasets demonstrate that our method
achieves competitive accuracy while maintaining high in-
ference speed (FPS), making it well-suited for edge-device
deployment in practical traffic surveillance systems.
1. Introduction
Object detection using fisheye cameras has gained increas-
ing attention in intelligent transportation and surveillance
systems due to the unique advantage of wide-angle cover-
age. Unlike conventional pinhole cameras, fisheye lenses
capture omnidirectional scenes with a single device, signif-
icantly reducing infrastructure cost and blind spots in com-
plex urban environments [3]. However, the extreme radial
distortion inherent in fisheye imagery introduces substan-
tial challenges for standard object detection models. Ob-
jects near the image center often appear relatively undis-
torted, while those at the periphery are compressed and
warped, resulting in severe scale variation, shape deforma-
tion, and inconsistent feature representation [9]. These fac-
tors lead to degraded detection accuracy, poor model con-
vergence during training, and substantial difficulty in de-
ploying real-time detection systems in practice. Addressing
these challenges requires detection frameworks that are not
only distortion-aware but also computationally efficient for
practical deployment in high-throughput scenarios such as
traffic monitoring.
Despite recent progress, existing solutions for fisheye
object detection exhibit several limitations that restrict their
practicality in real-world deployments. First, the heavy re-
liance on ensemble-based pipelines – combining multiple
large-scale models such as YOLOv9 [14], InternImage [15],
and Co-DETR [18] – demonstrates diminishing returns
when scaled for deployment on resource-constrained plat-
forms. These methods typically involve not only multiple
model forward passes but also post-processing techniques,
such as weighted box fusion (WBF) [10], which collectively
increase inference latency and memory usage [2, 9]. Conse-
quently, while high accuracy is achieved in benchmark set-
tings, these methods are fundamentally incompatible with
real-time constraints typical of traffic surveillance systems.
Second, most current approaches do not explicitly con-
sider the spatial characteristics of fisheye distortion during
either training or inference. In particular, objects at the im-
age periphery – which are often smaller, warped, and ap-
pear in higher density – are disproportionately harder to
detect and contribute more heavily to performance degra-

models may overfit to relatively clean center-region objects
while underperforming on the periphery, leading to poor

To bridge these gaps, we propose a unified framework
that integrates three core innovations tailored to the unique
challenges of fisheye object detection. First, we intro-
duce the Adaptive Periphery-Aware Module (APAM) ,
which injects spatial awareness into the training process
by dynamically weighting the loss based on radial dis-
tortion, enabling the model to prioritize learning from
distortion-heavy peripheral regions. Second, we present
Radial-Aware Data Augmentation (RADA), a geometry-
sensitive augmentation strategy that selectively pastes ob-
ject instances into highly distorted areas, thereby improving
the robustness and generalization of the detector. Finally,
to enable real-time deployment, we introduce GPU-Aware
Optimization (GAO), a TensorRT-based runtime strategy
that leverages pre-allocated GPU buffers, asynchronous ex-
ecution. Combined with mixed-precision and kernel fu-
sion, GAO significantly reduces latency and memory usage
for efficient edge deployment. Together, these components
deliver a distortion-aware, computationally efficient solu-
tion that meets the stringent performance demands of high-
throughput traffic monitoring applications.
The effectiveness of the proposed framework was further
demonstrated in the Fisheye Object Detection track of the
2025 AI City Challenge [11], where it achieved a competi-
tive F1-score of 0.6302. Importantly, this performance was
attained while maintaining high inference speed on edge
devices, underscoring the framework’s suitability for real-
time deployment in practical traffic surveillance scenarios
that demand both accuracy and computational efficiency.
2. Related Work
2.1. Fisheye Image Detection
Fisheye image detection poses significant challenges due
to the severe non-linear distortions inherent in ultra-wide-
angle lens imagery. Early approaches typically involved
geometric rectification, wherein fisheye images are re-
projected into rectilinear or panoramic formats prior to de-
tection. While this strategy enables the use of conventional
object detectors, it introduces several drawbacks: the com-
putational burden of re-projection, the risk of introducing
artifacts, and the distortion or loss of peripheral informa-
tion. These limitations make such methods less suitable
for real-time applications or scenarios with dynamic view-
points.
In response, recent research has shifted toward direct
detection on raw fisheye images by adapting the architec-
ture of convolutional neural networks (CNNs) to account
for spatial distortion. Notably, FisheyeDet [6] employs a
distortion-adaptive module that modifies convolutional ker-
nels based on each pixel’s distance from the image center,
enabling the network to extract features in a spatially-aware
manner. Similarly, FisheyeYOLO [4] explores alternative
object representations such as oriented ellipses and curved
bounding boxes to more accurately localize warped objects.
These methods are designed to handle the scale and shape
deformations prevalent in peripheral regions, where con-
ventional bounding boxes often fail to capture object ge-
ometry effectively.
Despite architectural innovations, detection performance
at the image periphery remains a persistent bottleneck. Ob-
jects in these regions tend to appear smaller, more dis-
torted, and more frequently occluded, resulting in higher
miss rates. Studies such as [5] underscore the dispropor-
tionate impact of peripheral detection quality on overall
performance, motivating further exploration into region-
aware feature extraction and learning strategies that prior-
itize distortion-sensitive zones of the fisheye image.
2.2. Real-time Efficient Detection
The landscape of real-time object detection is fundamen-
tally shaped by the dual demands of high-speed infer-
ence and stringent computational efficiency, particularly for
latency-sensitive applications such as autonomous naviga-
tion, intelligent surveillance, and mobile robotics. In re-
sponse to these constraints, the field has seen a prolifera-
tion of lightweight, high-throughput detection architectures.
Single-stage detectors, by virtue of their streamlined design,
have become the predominant approach for real-time tasks.
Within this paradigm, the You Only Look Once (YOLO)
family of models has been particularly influential. Initially
proposed by Redmon et al. [8], the YOLO framework rev-
olutionized real-time object detection by unifying the tra-
ditionally separate processes of region proposal and clas-
sification into a single, end-to-end neural network. This
foundational concept of treating object detection as a re-
gression problem allows for a single forward pass to pre-
dict both bounding boxes and class probabilities, thereby
achieving significant gains in inference speed. Subsequent
iterations, including the widely adopted YOLOv10 [13] and
YOLOv12 [12], have progressively refined this architec-
ture. These advancements have introduced more sophisti-
cated backbone networks for enhanced feature extraction,
anchor-free detection mechanisms to simplify the predic-
tion process, and the development of task-aligned head
modules, collectively contributing to substantial improve-
ments in both detection accuracy and computational perfor-
mance.
