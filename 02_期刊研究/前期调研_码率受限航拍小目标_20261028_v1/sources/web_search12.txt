## Abstract

With the rapid development of the low-altitude economy, real-time detection of low-altitude small targets has become a key technology in fields such as UAV inspection and intelligent security. However, existing detection models face problems such as insufficient small target feature expression, contradiction between lightweight and accuracy, and difficulty in edge deployment. To solve these problems, this study focuses on improving detection accuracy, optimizing model lightweight, and enhancing edge deployment efficiency. A lightweight low-altitude small target detection model based on improved YOLOv8n is proposed, which integrates depthwise separable convolution, Ghost module and partial convolution to optimize the backbone network, reducing model complexity while retaining small target feature extraction capability. An adaptive attention fusion mechanism is designed to strengthen small target feature expression and suppress complex background interference. Combined with structured pruning and INT8 quantization, the model compression strategy is implemented to improve edge deployment efficiency. Experimental results on VisDrone and UAVDT datasets show that the proposed model achieves 87.4% [email protected], 118 FPS and 2.4M parameters, outperforming mainstream comparison models. The model can be stably deployed on edge devices such as Jetson Nano and RK3588, and has broad application prospects in UAV inspection, intelligent transportation, urban security and border monitoring. Future work will focus on Transformer fusion, multi-modal detection and edge-cloud collaborative perception to further improve detection performance in complex scenarios.

## 1 Introduction

The low-altitude economy has experienced rapid growth in recent years, with continuously rising demand for applications such as drone usage, intelligent security monitoring, and automated inspection systems. 
The task of low-altitude small object detection faces multifaceted technical challenges, primarily concentrated across four dimensions. First is the issue of feature degradation: due to the minimal pixel presence of small objects in images, deep convolutional operations often lead to loss of detailed object information, compounded by their inherently weak texture features, resulting in higher false-negative rates and increased positioning inaccuracies during detection. Second is the challenge of complex background interference—common factors in low-altitude scenes such as cloud occlusion, architectural texture interference, variations in lighting intensity, and motion blur can cause false detections and feature confusion, compromising detection accuracy. Third is the trade-off between lightweight architecture and detection precision: while lightweight networks reduce model parameters and computational costs (FLOPs) by simplifying architectures, they compromise feature extraction capabilities and multi-scale object perception, making it difficult to balance lightweight requirements with detection accuracy. Third, to improve edge deployment efficiency by adapting the detection model to meet the hardware constraints of edge devices, enabling real-time detection, low-power operation, and high throughput deployment objectives, thereby facilitating the practical implementation of low-altitude small target detection technology.

This study clearly defines five core research objectives as follows: Objective 1 – Develop a lightweight low-altitude target detection network architecture tailored to the hardware constraints of edge devices; Objective 2 – Design an adaptive attention fusion mechanism to enhance feature extraction and discrimination capabilities for small targets; Objective 3 – Optimize multi-scale feature enhancement to improve detection performance for low-altitude targets across different scales; Objective 4 – Implement model compression through pruning and quantization techniques to reduce model complexity and resource consumption; Objective 5 – Achieve optimized real-time deployment on edge devices, ensuring the model meets real-time detection and low-power requirements.

The primary contributions of this study adhere to ACM/IEEE standard specifications as follows: Contribution 1 proposes an improved lightweight backbone network that effectively reduces model complexity through the integrated application of deeply separable convolutional layers and Ghost modules, while preserving critical feature information with reduced parameter count and computational requirements; Contribution 2 introduces an adaptive attention fusion module that dynamically adjusts feature weights to enhance small-object detection performance in complex scenarios and mitigate feature confusion; Contribution 3 establishes a multi-scale feature enhancement mechanism that improves the network's robustness for detecting low-altitude objects across different scales via multi-scale feature fusion and augmentation operations, minimizing detection errors caused by scale variations; Contribution 4 combines structured pruning with INT8 quantization optimization techniques to compress and optimize the detection model, enabling efficient deployment on edge devices while balancing detection accuracy and real-time performance.

## 4. Discussion

### 4.1. Discussion of the Results of VisDrone2019 Dataset

In terms of the lightweight characteristics of the model, Aero-YOLO26 achieves extreme compression of parameter count by virtue of the C2f_GhostECA lightweight feature extraction module, the SlimSPPF lightweight multi-scale pooling module, and the reconstruction of the backbone network and PAN-FPN feature fusion network. The nano variant Aero-YOLO26n requires only 1.99 M parameters, making it the smallest model among all evaluated detectors. Compared with other nano-scale baselines, the parameter count of Aero-YOLO26n represents 63.0% of YOLOv8n, 93.4% of YOLOv9t, 76.0% of YOLOv11n, and 77.4% of YOLO26n. The small variant Aero-YOLO26s contains 9.16 M parameters, which is substantially lighter than YOLOv8s, YOLOv11s, and YOLO26s, corresponding to 82.0%, 96.8%, and 91.5% of their sizes, respectively. When compared with specialized UAV-oriented lightweight detectors, Aero-YOLO26n is 34.8% smaller than Drone-YOLOn, 48.5% smaller than BAF-Net, and merely one-fourth the size of YOLO-RACE. Despite integrating stronger feature representation, Aero-YOLO26s maintains a parameter count that is still comparable to the smaller Drone-YOLOt and far below YOLO-RACE. When compared with medium-scale models such as YOLOv8m and YOLO26m, both Aero-YOLO26 variants exhibit an order of magnitude reduction in parameter count. Thus, the computational and storage overhead is greatly reduced while retaining strong multi-scale receptive fields and feature extraction capabilities.

In terms of core detection accuracy, Aero-YOLO26 demonstrates a significant breakthrough on the basis of an extremely lightweight design. The nano variant Aero-YOLO26n achieves the best performance among the evaluated nano models, with a precision of 0.500, recall of 0.395, mAP@50 of 0.382, and F1 score of 0.441. Horizontally compared with nano baselines, it surpasses YOLOv8n by 10.86% in precision, 12.2% in recall, 7.9% in mAP@50, and 11.9% in F1 score. Compared with YOLOv9t, the absolute mAP@50 improvement of Aero-YOLO26n reaches 3.8 pp with an almost identical parameter count. Against specialized UAV-oriented lightweight detectors, Aero-YOLO26n outperforms Drone-YOLOn by 2.1 pp in mAP@50 and achieves an F1 score 3.8 pp higher, while Aero-YOLO26n demonstrates improvements over BAF-Net by 4.2 pp in mAP@50 and 4.0 pp in F1 score. Aero-YOLO26n also surpasses YOLO-RACE by a substantial 6.6 pp in mAP@50 with only 24.9% of the parameters, underscoring its superior accuracy–efficiency trade-off. Aero-YOLO26s achieves the highest precision, recall, mAP@50, mAP50-95, and F1 score among all small models. Horizontally, compared with YOLOv8s, Aero-YOLO26s delivers a 4.1 pp absolute gain; compared with YOLOv9s, YOLOv11s, and YOLO26s, the improvements are 2.9 pp, 4.6 pp, and 3.1 pp, respectively. Notably, Aero-YOLO26s also surpasses the dedicated Drone-YOLOt by 3.4 pp in mAP@50 and by 1.8 pp in F1 score, while using a comparable parameter budget. In a vertical comparison across scales, Aero-YOLO26s outperforms the medium YOLOv8m with only 35.4% of the parameters, and closely approaches YOLOv9m, YOLOv11m, and YOLO26m, trailing the strongest medium model by merely 2.7 pp while saving 58.2% of the parameters. Even when compared with the two-stage anchor-based detectors, Aero-YOLO26n already substantially outperforms Fast R-CNN and Mask R-CNN with less than 4.5% of their parameters, and Aero-YOLO26s delivers an even larger absolute mAP@50 improvement of over 12.7 pp against these traditional architectures. These gains are attributable to the C2f_GhostECA module, whose GhostBottleneck_ECA and EECA attention amplify multi-scale feature responses critical for small aerial objects, and to the SlimSPPF module, which maintains full multi-scale receptive field coverage and removes redundant intermediate feature memory overhead via pre-channel compression.

The stark performance gap between the two-stage anchor-based models (Fast R-CNN, Mask R-CNN) and the Aero-YOLO26 series can be fundamentally attributed to the divergence between anchor-based and anchor-free detection paradigms. Fast R-CNN and Mask R-CNN rely on densely pre-defined anchor boxes of various scales and aspect ratios to generate region proposals, which are then refined through a second-stage regression and classification. In UAV imagery, where objects exhibit extreme scale variation, heavy occlusion, and a predominance of small targets, such hand-crafted anchors struggle to provide adequate coverage and lead to severe foreground–background imbalance. Consequently, these methods incur massive computational overhead yet yield very low mAP@50. In contrast, Aero-YOLO26 adopts an anchor-free detection strategy, which discards the predefined anchor boxes and instead directly predicts object centers or key points along with their bounding box offsets. Thus, the design removes the sensitive hyperparameter tuning of anchors, simplifies the detection head, and naturally accommodates objects of arbitrary shape and scale aligned with the attributes of aerial targets. Coupled with the lightweight C2f_GhostECA and SlimSPPF modules, the anchor-free Aero-YOLO26n achieves a 26.9% higher mAP@50 than Fast R-CNN and a 21.7% higher mAP@50 than Mask R-CNN, while reducing the parameter count by more than 95%, and Aero-YOLO26s attains an mAP@50 12.7 pp higher than the best two-stage competitor. 
With respect to per-class performance, Aero-YOLO26n attains the highest AP on six of the nine VisDrone2019 categories. The gains are particularly pronounced on pedestrian and van; compared with the strongest competing nano model, the AP of pedestrian improves by 4.8 pp over YOLO26n, and the AP of van improves by 4.4 pp over YOLOv8n, underscoring the effectiveness of the EECA module in suppressing background clutter and highlighting small-scale objects. In contrast, Aero-YOLO26s attains the highest AP on five of the nine VisDrone2019 categories. The gains are particularly substantial on pedestrian and van, underscoring the effectiveness of the EECA attention in suppressing background clutter and highlighting small-scale objects. For both Aero-YOLO26n and Aero-YOLO26s, a modest decrease is observed on a few extremely tiny categories such as bicycle and awning tricycle, which aligns with the typical trade-off of lightweight architectures where the extreme parameter compression slightly attenuates the representation of severely scale-limited instances. Crucially, the dominant aerial categories that drive overall detection performance, such as car and pedestrian, exhibit clear and significant improvements.

Although the VisDrone2019 dataset provides a standardized basis for model comparison, its results alone cannot fully characterize the performance of real-world aerial photography scenarios. Therefore, the results of the UAV-MCT dataset are expected to be discussed as follows.

## Abstract

Accurate small-object detection is crucial for automated processes in remote sensing imagery, such as environmental monitoring and aerial surveillance. Yet it remains challenging due to limited feature representation, complex backgrounds, and localization errors. To address these challenges, this work proposes a lightweight detection approach that integrates a scale-based dynamic loss and efficient multi-scale attention. A lightweight backbone, dubbed A2C2fLite, is designed based on the A2C2f module from the most recent YOLO variant to reduce redundant parameters and computational overhead. An EMA module is embedded before the final detection heads to enhance multi-scale feature representation and improve target-background discrimination. The SD loss is used to effectively address scale variation and occlusion, improving localization accuracy beyond existing Intersection-over-Union-based losses. Evaluated on a representative remote sensing dataset, the proposed approach achieves 93.6% precision, 86.6% recall, and 92.6% ${{\textstyle \text{mAP}}}_{50}$ $\hbox {mAP}_{50}$, with only 2.0M parameters and 5.7 GFLOPs. Compared to the baseline YOLOv13, it achieves a superior accuracy-efficiency trade-off by reducing parameter counts by 20% and computational overhead by 8.1%, while improving ${{\textstyle \text{mAP}}}_{50}$ $\hbox {mAP}_{50}$ by 1.5%. Visualization results demonstrate that it suppresses missed alarms and refines bounding-box localization. By minimizing memory footprint and computational bottlenecks, this lightweight design provides clear practical advantages for real-time small-object detection on resource-constrained edge devices, such as onboard Unmanned Aerial Vehicle systems and embedded aerial sensors. These findings highlight that the proposed approach offers an efficient, robust, and highly deployable solution for complex remote sensing applications.

### 3.1. Dataset, Small-Object Definition, and Robustness Protocol

Two complementary benchmarks were used. In this paper, corruption robustness refers specifically to performance under the controlled photometric transformations defined below; it does not by itself imply robustness to real weather or unseen sensing modalities. The VisDrone2021-based benchmark retains the UAV source-domain scene distribution, whereas SODA-D-Robustness adds a ground-view driving-domain shift. Both use Gaussian noise [29], camera-pipeline/ISO-like noise [30], brightness and contrast variation, rain, fog, shadow, snow, and sunlight flare; random combinations form the mixed-corruption condition. These transformations do not alter geometry, so the original boxes remain valid. The VisDrone protocol yields 3584 derived images, with the unmodified images used as the Original control subset.

Following the COCO convention [31], object size is defined by the area of each ground-truth bounding box in the original VisDrone annotation coordinates before network resizing: small below 1024 pixels^{2}, medium from 1024 to below 9216 pixels^{2}, and large at or above 9216 pixels^{2}, corresponding to square-side thresholds of 32 and 96 pixels. 
Applying the shared corruption pipeline to every SODA-D test image produces SODA-D-Robustness, which contains eight single-corruption variants and one mixed-corruption variant per source image. The mixed variant combines two or three randomly selected transformations at a strength of 0.55. Generation uses the fixed seed 20,260,807, and transformed images are stored at JPEG quality 92. In total, the dataset contains 66,852 corrupted images across nine conditions, together with 7428 unmodified control images and unchanged bounding-box annotations. To avoid treating correlated variants of the same scene as independent samples, Table 3 uses only the 7428-image mixed-corruption split for the cross-dataset comparison.

## Abstract

Space debris presents a growing operational risk to spacecraft, especially in low Earth orbit, where collisions can generate further debris and increase future collision probability. Active debris removal and in-orbit servicing require robust close-range perception, but on-board systems are constrained by power, memory, processing capability and the need for reliable real-time operation.
