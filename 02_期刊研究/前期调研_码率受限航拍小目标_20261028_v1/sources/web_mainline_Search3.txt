## 4 Results

### 4.1 Dataset Preparation

VisDrone2019 [47] is an image database developed by a team at Tianjin University, featuring imagery captured from drone perspectives. It comprises a total of 8,629 images of varying sizes, including 6,471 annotated training images, 548 annotated validation images, and 1,610 annotated testing images. The dataset encompasses 12 image categories: ignored regions, pedestrians, people, bicycles, cars, vans, trucks, tricycles, motor tricycles, buses, motorcycles and others. The objects within these images are characterised by their high density and small size. Furthermore, the images are sourced from diverse locations across the country, resulting in varying backgrounds, scales, densities and lighting conditions. The randomness and uncertainty present in this dataset pose significant challenges to the performance of models, but they also provide an excellent opportunity to train models that are more robust and accurate in complex scenarios. As illustrated in Figure 7, the most prevalent category is car targets, followed by pedestrian targets, while categories such as tricycles, trucks and bicycles account for a smaller proportion. --------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Several challenges remain open. First, although the residual-prior path is lightweight relative to the detector backbone, deployment on embedded drone platforms still requires pruning, quantization, or early-exit designs. Second, the present framework uses RGB reconstruction discrepancies; future work may incorporate temporal cues, event cameras, or hyperspectral/multimodal sensing to make the prior more reliable under low contrast and heavy clutter. Third, uncertainty-aware priors could further distinguish true evidence deficiency from background artefacts, especially in scenes with repeated high-frequency textures. Finally, extending the same evidence-recovery formulation to transformer-based detectors and open-vocabulary aerial detection is a promising direction for broader drone-view perception.
