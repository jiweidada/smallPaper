Focal and Global Knowledge Distillation for Detectors     

Zhendong Yang, Zhe Li, Xiaohu Jiang, Yuan Gong, Zehuan Yuan, Danpei Zhao, Chun Yuan; Proceedings of the IEEE/CVF Conference on Computer Vision and Pattern Recognition (CVPR), 2022, pp. 4643-4652

Abstract

Knowledge distillation has been applied to image classification successfully. However, object detection is much more sophisticated and most knowledge distillation methods have failed on it. In this paper, we point out that in object detection, the features of the teacher and student vary greatly in different areas, especially in the foreground and background. If we distill them equally, the uneven differences between feature maps will negatively affect the distillation. Thus, we propose Focal and Global Distillation (FGD). Focal distillation separates the foreground and background, forcing the student to focus on the teacher's critical pixels and channels. Global distillation rebuilds the relation between different pixels and transfers it from teachers to students, compensating for missing global information in focal distillation. As our method only needs to calculate the loss on the feature map, FGD can be applied to various detectors. We experiment on various detectors with different backbones and the results show that the student detector achieves excellent mAP improvement. For example, ResNet-50 based RetinaNet, Faster RCNN, RepPoints and Mask RCNN with our distillation method achieve 40.7%, 42.0%, 42.0% and 42.1% mAP on COCO2017, which are 3.3, 3.6, 3.4 and 2.9 higher than the baseline, respectively. Our codes are available at https://github.com/yzd-v/FGD.

## 1. Introduction

Recently, deep learning has achieved great success in various domains [8, 9, 22, 24]. To get better performance, we usually use a larger backbone, which needs more compute resources and inferences more slowly. To get over this, knowledge distillation has been proposed [11]. Knowledge distillation is a method to inherit the information from a large teacher network to a compact student network and achieve strong performance without extra cost during inference time. However, most distillation methods [10, 27, 33, 34] are designed for image classification, which lead to trivial improvements for object detection.

# FSIC: Frequency-separated image compression for small object detection

## Abstract

The existing image compression methods are designed for the human visual system. They can achieve good compression quality for low-frequency components of the image that are important to human vision. However, for object detection models, both high and low-frequency components are essential. As a result, the detection metrics on the compressed images obtained by current methods will decline. Particularly for small object detection, the lack of high-frequency signals makes it difficult to distinguish the targets from the background. In this paper, we propose a frequency-separated image compression model, named FSIC. During the training process, the compression of low-frequency components only employs MSE loss, while the compression of high-frequency components additionally incorporates a detection loss. Fritsche et al. [12] proposed to separate the low-frequency components (LFC) and high-frequency components (HFC) of the input image when performing image super-resolution reconstruction with neural networks and train them in different ways. In this work, we first investigate the compression performance of existing compression networks on LFC and HFC using object detection task results as the evaluation metric. Then we propose the frequency-separated image compression model (FSIC) which extends the joint training framework by incorporating a frequency separation learning strategy to train the compression network.

We conduct frequency separation on the VisDrone dataset [13] and utilize the existing learning-based compression method [6] to compress the original image, LFC, and HFC respectively. Then we use YOLOv5 [14] to perform detection on them and get the following findings:

  * •

Both the LFC and HFC of an image are utilized by the object detection model.

  * •

Existing learning-based compression methods using MSE as the loss function demonstrate favorable compression performances on LFC, but their compression performances on HFC are not satisfactory.

Based on the above findings, we propose FSIC. Specifically, we employ a linear filter to separate the LFC and HFC of the input image in the training process. Then, the MSE loss is used to guide the compression of both LFC and HFC. The compression of HFC is further optimized through joint training with a detection model. FSIC exhibits excellent compression performance for object detection tasks on the small object dataset. For extremely high compression ratios, our model has advantages compared with existing compression methods. Moreover, the encoding speed of FSIC is faster than that of the compared learning-based compression models.

## Datasets

In object detection, objects smaller than 32×32 pixels are considered small objects. To verify the compression performance of FSIC for small object detection tasks, we compare it with other image compression methods on two classic small object detection datasets, VisDrone [13] and DOTA [37]. The images in the dataset are scaled to 640×640 to avoid the impact of image resizing in the detection model.

So the compression performance on small object detection tasks is poor. Therefore, we propose a compression model (FSIC) that incorporates frequency separation. ### What problem does this paper attempt to solve?

The paper addresses the limited effectiveness of conventional knowledge distillation for object detection. Knowledge distillation has worked well for image classification, where a student model can imitate the teacher’s output logits or intermediate representations. Object detection is more difficult because its feature maps simultaneously encode foreground objects, background regions, multiple object scales, spatial locations, channels, and global relationships among pixels.

The paper identifies three closely related problems:

  1. Foreground–background imbalance. Detection feature maps contain many more background pixels than foreground pixels. 
The central problem is therefore how to transfer useful knowledge from a large teacher detector to a smaller student detector while avoiding noisy, imbalanced, and uniformly weighted feature matching. The proposed solution must also be general enough to work with two-stage detectors, anchor-based one-stage detectors, anchor-free one-stage detectors, and instance-segmentation models.

### What related research exists?

#### Object detection architectures

The paper situates its method within three major CNN-based detection paradigms:

  * Two-stage detectors, such as Faster RCNN and Mask RCNN, which use a Region Proposal Network followed by an RCNN head. They generally achieve strong accuracy but require more computation.
  * Anchor-based one-stage detectors, such as RetinaNet, SSD, and YOLOv3, which directly predict classification and localization outputs from feature maps using predefined anchor boxes.
  * Anchor-free one-stage detectors, such as FCOS, RepPoints, and CenterNet, which predict object locations or key points without predefined anchor boxes.

Although their detection heads differ, all these systems consume feature maps. This motivates the paper’s feature-level distillation design, which can be inserted at the feature or neck level without depending on the specific detection head.

#### Knowledge distillation for classification

The paper reviews the origins of knowledge distillation in image classification:

  * Hinton et al. introduced the use of teacher outputs as soft labels to transfer “dark knowledge” from a large model to a smaller one.
  * FitNet demonstrated that intermediate feature representations can also guide the student.
  * Attention-transfer, feature-transformation, and similarity-based methods further improved classification distillation by emphasizing feature importance or relational structure.

However, the paper argues that these approaches cannot be transferred directly to detection because detection features have severe foreground–background imbalance and contain spatially heterogeneous information.

# CVPR 2022 | 针对目标检测的重点与全局知识蒸馏(FGD)

发布人：计算机视觉工坊 时间：2022-03-19 来源：工程师

作者丨美索不达米亚平原@知乎（已授权）

来源丨https://zhuanlan.zhihu.com/p/477707304编辑丨极市平台

本文介绍我们CVPR2022关于目标检测的知识蒸馏工作: Focal and Global Knowledge Distillation for Detectors，只需要30行代码就可以在anchor-base, anchor-free的单阶段、两阶段各种检测器上稳定涨点，现在代码已经开源，欢迎大家试用～

文章链接：https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.11837

Image: 图片一、针对问题1. 目标检测中前背景不平衡问题

前背景的不平衡对于目标检测而言是一个重要的问题，这个问题同样影响着知识蒸馏。

知识蒸馏旨在使学生学习教师的知识，以获得相似的输出从而提升性能。为了探索学生与教师在特征层面的差异，我们首先对二者的特征图进行了可视化。可以看到在空间与通道注意力上，教师与学生均存在较大的差异。其中在空间注意力上，二者在前景中的差异较大，在背景中的差异较小，这会给蒸馏中的学生带来不同的学习难度。

Image: 图片

为了进一步探索前背景对于知识蒸馏的影响，我们分离出前背景进行了蒸馏实验，全图一起蒸馏会导致蒸馏性能的下降，将前景与背景分开学生能够获得更好的表现。

Image: 图片

针对学生与教师注意力的差异，前景与背景的差异，我们提出了重点蒸馏Focal Distillation：分离前背景，并利用教师的空间与通道注意力作为权重，指导学生进行知识蒸馏，计算重点蒸馏损失。

2.全局信息的丢失

如前所述，Focal Distillation将前景与背景分开进行蒸馏，割断了前背景的联系，缺乏了特征的全局信息的蒸馏。为此，我们提出了全局蒸馏Global Distillation：利用GcBlock分别提取学生与教师的全局信息，并进行全局蒸馏损失的计算。

Image: 图片二、整体框架

FGD仅需要获取学生与教师的特征图，便可完成重点蒸馏损失与全局蒸馏损失的计算，可以很方便的应用到各种类型的检测器上。

Image: 图片三、实验结果

我们对anchor-based与anchor-free的单阶段与二阶段检测器进行了实验，在COCO2017上学生检测器均获得了大幅的AP和AR提升。

Image: 图片

我们采用了具有更强的检测器对学生进行蒸馏，发现当使用更强的模型作为教师进行蒸馏时，FGD为模型能带来更大的性能的提升。例如RetinaNet-R50在ResNet-101和ResNeXt-101的老师蒸馏下，分别可达到39.7和40.7的mAP。

Image: 图片

对于使用FGD蒸馏完成后的学生模型，我们再次进行了注意力的可视化。可以看到，经过FGD训练后的学生，空间注意力和通道注意力的分布与教师都非常相似，这表明学生通过蒸馏学到了教师的知识并获得了更好的特征，由此实现了性能的提升。
