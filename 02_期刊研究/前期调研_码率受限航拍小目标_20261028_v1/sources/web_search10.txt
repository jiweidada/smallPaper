# CUVi: Compression Aware Underwater Visual Intelligence for Bandwidth Constrained Object Detection

27 Pages Posted: 16 Jul 2026

## Sohail Asghar

### Abstract

Autonomous underwater vehicles must transmit imagery over acoustic links at 1– 10 kbps, yet standard compression discards exactly the information underwater detectors need, and a long-running debate over whether image enhancement helps or hurts detection remains unresolved because no existing tool reveals which pixels a detector actually uses. We introduce CUVi, a compression-aware visual intelligence framework that jointly trains a learned encoder, decoder, and detection head through a unified rate– distortion–task loss, so that detection gradients directly shape bit allocation during encoding. On a deduplicated, class-stratified split of the URPC2020 underwater dataset, CUVi reaches mAP@0.5 of 0.300 at 0.175 bpp, within 0.004 mAP of full-resolution detection, with the task-gradient pathway responsible for the largest single gain in an ablation study. The resulting spatial bit-allocation maps give the first per-class, pixellevel evidence of what a detector relies on: boundary cues for camouflaged organisms such as holothurians, and interior texture for spinous organisms such as echinus. This class-conditional picture resolves why generic image enhancement inflates false positives for some classes while leaving others unaffected, and it identifies which visual cues a bandwidth-constrained pipeline must preserve. A deployment analysis further shows that CUVi raises the achievable frame rate over standard codecs at matched detection quality on acoustic and radio-frequency communication links representative of real autonomous underwater vehicle deployments.

Keywords: Underwater object detection, Learned image compression, rate--distortion--task optimisation, Autonomous Underwater Vehicles, bandwidth-constrained inference, URPC2020

## Market Demand for UAV Detection Systems

The proliferation of unmanned aerial vehicles across commercial, defense, and civilian sectors has catalyzed substantial growth in the UAV detection systems market. ## Current UAV Compression and Detection Challenges

UAV systems face mounting pressure to transmit high-resolution imagery and video data through bandwidth-constrained communication channels, particularly in remote sensing, surveillance, and disaster response applications. Current compression techniques, while effective at reducing data volume, introduce artifacts and information loss that can significantly degrade the performance of automated detection algorithms. The fundamental challenge lies in balancing transmission efficiency against the preservation of critical visual features required for accurate object identification and classification.
Existing compression standards such as JPEG, H.264, and H.265 were primarily designed for human visual perception rather than machine vision tasks. These codecs often prioritize perceptual quality metrics that do not align with the feature preservation requirements of modern deep learning detection models. Consequently, compressed UAV imagery may appear visually acceptable to human observers while containing subtle distortions that severely impact detection accuracy, particularly for small objects or targets with low contrast against complex backgrounds.
The computational constraints of onboard UAV processing present additional complications. Real-time compression must operate within strict power and processing budgets, limiting the complexity of encoding algorithms that can be deployed. This constraint becomes particularly acute when UAVs must perform edge computing tasks simultaneously, forcing trade-offs between compression quality, detection processing, and flight control operations.
Current detection frameworks lack standardized methodologies for quantifying how different compression parameters affect accuracy metrics across diverse operational scenarios. Researchers and practitioners struggle to establish optimal compression settings that maintain acceptable detection performance while meeting bandwidth limitations. The absence of comprehensive benchmarks comparing compression impacts across various detection architectures, target types, and environmental conditions further complicates system optimization efforts.
Latency requirements add another layer of complexity, as many UAV applications demand near-instantaneous detection responses. The cumulative delay introduced by compression, transmission, decompression, and detection processing must remain within acceptable thresholds, constraining the selection of compression algorithms and parameter configurations. This temporal constraint often forces operators to accept higher compression ratios than ideal, potentially sacrificing detection accuracy for operational responsiveness.

## Standardization and Interoperability Requirements

The effective deployment of compressed UAV data for detection tasks across diverse operational environments necessitates robust standardization frameworks and interoperability protocols. Currently, the absence of unified standards governing compression algorithms, data formats, and quality metrics creates significant barriers to seamless integration across multi-vendor UAV systems and detection platforms. Establishing comprehensive standardization requirements becomes critical when quantifying compression impact, as inconsistent implementation approaches can introduce variability that confounds accurate performance assessment.
Industry-wide standardization efforts must address multiple technical dimensions. Compression ratio definitions require uniform calculation methodologies to enable meaningful cross-platform comparisons. Hardware-accelerated compression solutions using dedicated encoding chips or FPGA implementations have emerged as essential enablers, achieving processing speeds of 30-60 frames per second for 4K video streams. However, these hardware solutions often sacrifice compression efficiency compared to software-based approaches, creating a fundamental trade-off between processing speed and data reduction effectiveness.
Adaptive compression strategies have gained prominence as methods to dynamically balance bandwidth constraints against detection accuracy requirements. These approaches adjust compression parameters based on real-time network conditions, scene complexity, and mission priorities. Region-of-interest encoding techniques allocate higher bitrates to areas containing potential targets while applying aggressive compression to background regions, optimizing bandwidth utilization without uniformly degrading detection capabilities across the entire frame.
Edge computing architectures are increasingly deployed to mitigate bandwidth constraints by performing preliminary detection and filtering operations onboard the UAV platform. This approach transmits only relevant detections or compressed regions of interest rather than full-resolution imagery, potentially reducing bandwidth requirements by 80-95% while preserving critical detection information. The effectiveness of this strategy depends heavily on the accuracy of onboard detection algorithms and their computational efficiency within the UAV's limited processing resources.--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Our paper makes several contributions. Firstly, we introduce Compression-Aware Training (CAT), a novel technique for memory bandwidth reduction. The method works by introducing a loss term that penalizes the entropy of the activations at training time and by applying entropy encoding (e.g., Huffman coding) on the resulting activations at inference time. # Compression-Robust YOLOv7 for Smart Aquaculture Monitoring

## Overview

This project investigates how video compression affects YOLOv7 object detection performance in smart aquaculture monitoring systems.

The objective is to determine the maximum acceptable compression level that reduces bandwidth and storage requirements while maintaining reliable shrimp detection accuracy.

In addition to evaluating compression effects, this work explores different training strategies for improving robustness under severe compression, including Fixed-QP Training, Mixed-QP Training, and Curriculum Learning.

## Highlights

  * Evaluated YOLOv7 under 22 compression levels (QP15–QP51)
  * Compared three Fixed-QP training models
  * Developed Mixed-QP training strategy
  * Proposed Curriculum Learning over compression levels
  * Achieved nearly 4× bandwidth reduction
  * Improved robustness under severe compression conditions

# Research Motivation

Large-scale shrimp farms continuously generate video streams for:

  * Shrimp counting
  * Behavior monitoring
  * Feeding analysis
  * Long-term recording

Challenges:

  * Limited internet bandwidth
  * Large storage requirements
  * High transmission costs

Video compression provides an effective solution, but excessive compression may remove important visual features required for object detection.

This project investigates the trade-off between:

  * Detection Accuracy
  * Bandwidth Consumption
  * Storage Efficiency

# Experimental Pipeline

The overall workflow of the compression-performance evaluation.

Workflow:

  1. Original videos are compressed into different QP levels.
  2. Frames are extracted from compressed videos.
  3. YOLOv7 performs object detection.
  4. Detection performance is evaluated using mAP and IoU.
  5. Results are compared across compression levels.
