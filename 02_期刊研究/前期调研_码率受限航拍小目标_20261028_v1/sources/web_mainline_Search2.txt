## Abstract

Indoor object detection in real-world deployments often suffers from severe performance degradation due to complex structural corruptions such as motion blur, sensor noise, and compression artifacts, which are exacerbated by low-resolution capture in resource-constrained settings. This performance drop poses a critical challenge for applications in the interior design sector, including virtual home staging, intelligent furniture recognition, and AR/VR interior design experiences. Existing solutions either rely on computationally expensive super-resolution (SR) preprocessing pipelines or employ degradation-agnostic knowledge distillation (KD), the latter being limited by its inability to address different degradation types in a targeted manner. In this work, we introduce progressive degradation-aware distillation (PDAD), a zero-inference-overhead training framework that equips detectors with strong robustness against diverse degradations. PDAD first learns a degradation-aware proxy that captures the degradation characteristics of the input, and then uses this proxy as conditional guidance to adaptively refine detection features. All auxiliary components are discarded after training, leaving the deployed model identical in architecture and speed to its baseline counterpart. Extensive experiments on indoor datasets such as HomeObjects-3K show that PDAD achieves substantial gains over state-of-the-art (SOTA) methods under challenging mixed degradations and generalizes well to real-world indoor imagery. This robust capability is particularly crucial for real-world applications in interior design, such as automated inventory, space layout planning, and AR-based visualization. By providing a low-cost yet effective solution for robust indoor object detection, PDAD facilitates the widespread adoption of AI in resource-constrained design settings.

## Abstract

In recent years, knowledge distillation has been proved to be an effective solution for model compression. This approach can make lightweight student models acquire the knowledge extracted from cumbersome teacher models. However, previous distillation methods of detection have weak generalization for different detection frameworks and rely heavily on ground truth (GT), ignoring the valuable relation information between instances. ## 1. Introduction

In recent years, the accuracy of object detection has made a great progress due to the blossom of deep convolutional neural network (CNN). The deep learning network structure, including a variety of one-stage detection models [19, 23, 24, 25, 17] and two-stage detection models [26, 16, 8, 2], has replaced the traditional object detection and has become the mainstream method in this field. Furthermore, the anchor-free frameworks [13, 5, 32] have also achieved better performance with more simplified approaches. However, these high-precision deep learning based models are usually cumbersome, while a lightweight with high performance model is demanded in practical applications. Therefore, how to find a better trade-off between the accuracy and efficiency has become a crucial problem.

Knowledge Distillation (KD), proposed by Hinton et al. [10], is a promising solution for the above problem. Knowledge distillation is to transfer the knowledge of large model to small model, thereby improving the performance of the small model and achieving the purpose of model compression. At present, the typical forms of knowledge can be divided into three categories [7], response-based knowledge [10, 22], feature-based knowledge [27, 35, 9] and relation-based knowledge [22, 20, 31, 33, 15]. ## 3 Method

### 3.1 Overall Framework

The overall architecture of the proposed framework is illustrated in Figure 1. It consists of four key components: a feature extraction module, a DAM, a DA-RES, and a detection module. The input to the model is an aerial image captured from the drone view, which is often subject to adverse environmental conditions such as rain, snow, fog, and low-light nighttime scenarios, as well as limitations in the imaging hardware itself. These factors introduce varying degrees of image degradation that significantly interfere with reliable object detection. The central objective of the proposed methodology is to explicitly model these degradation patterns and integrate them into a domain-adaptive detection framework. To extract discriminative image features, we first employ a lightweight vision backbone (MobileNetV3 [39]) to obtain multiscale deep representations of the input image. The extracted features are then processed in two parallel streams: one branch passes through an average pooling layer to capture cross-channel global representations, while the other branch preserves high-frequency details via a skip connection. Both branches are subsequently transformed into the frequency domain using the Fast Fourier Transform (FFT), from which the amplitude spectra are obtained. By comparing the amplitude differences between individual feature channels and the global pooled spectrum, DAM can model degradation patterns that characterize the frequency-domain signatures of adverse conditions. In this way, degradation modeling is formulated as an explicit and learnable process embedded within the detection pipeline, rather than an external enhancement step. This enables the network to perceive degradation-specific variations. To further address the domain shift between clean and degraded samples, we propose a DA-RES. DA-RES is designed with three sets of encoders: a source-domain encoder for clean images, a target-domain encoder for degraded images, and a domain-shared encoder for learning invariant features. This encoder design reflects the methodological principle of disentangling domain-invariant semantics from domain-specific degradation effects, which is essential for robust cross-domain generalization. The degradation pattern estimated by DAM is embedded into the target-domain encoder to endow it with specific degradation discrimination capabilities. The outputs of the target-domain encoder are then combined with those of the shared encoder via an element-wise interaction to effectively suppress category-irrelevant information while enhancing domain-specific useful cues. During training, both source-domain and target-domain invariant features are simultaneously fed into a decoder, which enhances the model's ability to distinguish fine-grained cross-domain differences and strengthens feature restoration capacity.

### 3.2 DAM

The DAM is designed to explicitly characterize degradation cues in the frequency domain, inspired by the empirical observation that adverse-weather artifacts are predominantly encoded in the amplitude spectrum [40]. The methodological motivation behind DAM is to transform visually complex and heterogeneous degradation effects into structured and discriminative frequency-domain representations that can be learned by the network. As shown in Figure 2, swapping the amplitude spectra between degraded image pairs results in a clear transfer of degradation patterns, while the phase spectrum remains relatively stable. This indicates that amplitude variations provide a reliable signature of image degradations across weather types, motivating the DAM design. Formally, given an intermediate deep feature map $$$ F\in {R}^{C\times H\times W} $$$, we first compute its channel-wise average to obtain a cross-channel global descriptor:

$$$$ g=\mathrm{AVG}(F)\in {R}^{1\times H\times W} $$$$ (1)

FIGURE 2

Open in figure viewerPowerPoint

Illustration of the degradation transfer phenomenon. Swapping amplitude spectra between clean and degraded image pairs reveals that degradation cues are predominantly encoded in the frequency-domain amplitude.

Both F and g are transformed into the frequency domain via the FFT:

$$$$ {A}_g=\psi \left(\mathrm{FFT}(g)\right),{A}_f=\psi \left(\mathrm{FFT}(F)\right) $$$$ (2)

where $$$ \psi \left(\cdot \right) $$$ extracts the amplitude spectrum of its input. We then isolate degradation-specific components by subtracting the global amplitude spectrum from the channel-wise spectra:

$$$$ {A}_d={A}_f-{A}_g,{A}_d\in {R}^{C\times H\times W} $$$$ (3)

The residual amplitude spectrum $$$ {A}_d $$$ highlights frequency-domain variations that are tightly linked to degradations. By subtracting the global amplitude spectrum from the channel-wise amplitude responses, Equation (3) explicitly isolates channel-dependent residuals that are sensitive to degradation-induced frequency distortions, while suppressing domain-invariant semantic structures. This residual formulation forms the mathematical basis of DAM in the proposed method. To enhance its discriminative capacity, we apply a series of residual dense blocks (RDBs) with dilated convolutions, followed by a GELU activation:

$$$$ {M}_{\mathrm{mod}}=\mathrm{GELU}\left(\mathrm{RDB}\left({A}_d\kern0em \right)\right) $$$$ (4)

where $$$ {M}_{\mathrm{mod}}\in {R}^{C\times H\times W} $$$ serves as an activation map emphasizing degradation-sensitive regions. Next, $$$ {M}_{\mathrm{mod}} $$$ is used as a modulation signal on the original feature map:

$$$$ {F}_d={M}_{\mathrm{mod}}\odot F $$$$ (5)

Here, the element-wise modulation $$$ \odot $$$ acts as a degradation-guided feature recalibration mechanism, mathematically enforcing the network to amplify degradation-relevant responses while attenuating irrelevant background features. This design enables degradation priors to be explicitly injected into spatial-domain representations, allowing the network to perceive weather-specific distortions in context.

To further capture the probabilistic distribution of degradation types, $$$ {F}_d $$$ is processed by a lightweight multilayer perceptron (MLP) followed by a SoftMax function:

$$$$ \mathrm{score}=\mathrm{SoftMax}\left(\mathrm{MLP}\left({F}_d\kern0em \right)\right) $$$$ (6)

The SoftMax normalization ensures that the degradation score vector ($$$ \mathrm{score}\in {R}^K $$$) forms a valid probabilistic distribution over expert models, allowing the framework to softly route features to K experts. Each expert $$$ {\theta}_k $$$ specializes in addressing a distinct degradation pattern (e.g., rain, snow, fog). This design allows DAM to handle both single and compound degradations in a unified and data-driven manner.

In summary, the proposed DAM operates through three tightly coupled stages. First, it performs frequency-domain decomposition, where channel-wise residual spectra are used to isolate degradation-specific amplitude cues. Second, it applies activation modulation, leveraging RDBs and element-wise modulation to enhance discriminative degradation features within the deep representations. Finally, it conducts expert routing, in which the modulated features are mapped to a degradation score vector that dynamically selects specialized experts for handling diverse degradation types. Through this unified process, DAM enables the network to explicitly encode degradation signatures into the detection pipeline, thereby improving robustness under complex adverse-weather conditions and facilitating reliable UAV object detection across diverse scenarios. Together, these stages constitute a complete methodological pipeline for explicit degradation modeling within UAV object detection.

### 3.3 DA-RES

Although the DAM provides valuable cues about the types of degradations present in UAV imagery, a single fixed restoration pathway is insufficient to cope with the diversity and coexistence of adverse-weather effects. From a methodological standpoint, this motivates the need for a flexible restoration mechanism that can adaptively respond to multiple degradation patterns and domain shifts. The proposed DA-RES leverages the degradation score vector predicted by DAM to dynamically activate specialized experts, and then refines and fuses their outputs through domain-aware attention to obtain robust restored features, as shown in Figure 3. Formally, given an intermediate feature map $$$ F\in {R}^{C\times H\times W} $$$, DAM estimates a degradation score vector. Instead of routing exclusively to a single expert, the top-M experts with highest scores are selected to address potential mixtures of degradations. This multiexpert strategy reflects the practical observation that UAV images often suffer from compound degradations rather than isolated weather effects. Each expert $$$ {\theta}_k $$$ processes the input independently, producing candidate restorations. To achieve this, DA-RES introduces a domain-aware attention mechanism consisting of two complementary components, as shown in Figure 1c. The first, interdomain attention, emphasizes invariant structural cues by projecting each feature into a latent space and computing attention weights relative to domain-shared semantic embeddings:

$$$$ {\alpha}_k=\mathrm{SoftMax}\left(\phi \left({W}_cF,C\right)\right) $$$$ (7)

where $$$ {W}_c $$$ is a projection matrix, $$$ C $$$ encodes semantic information common across domains, and $$$ \phi \left(\cdot \right) $$$ denotes cosine similarity. This suppresses redundant background information while highlighting transferable object-related patterns.

FIGURE 3

Open in figure viewerPowerPoint

Overall workflow of the proposed DA-RES for adaptive feature restoration and fusion.

In contrast, intradomain attention explicitly preserves domain-specific variations. For each domain candidate features are adapted through a domain-specific projection $$$ {W}_T $$$ and evaluated against degeneration embeddings $$$ {\theta}_k $$$:

$$$$ {\beta}_k=\mathrm{SoftMax}\left(\phi \left({W}_TF,{\theta}_k\kern0em \right)\right) $$$$ (8)

This allows DA-RES to retain fine-grained cues reflecting degradation patterns. The restored features are then reconstructed through a decoder.

$$$$ {R}_k=\mathrm{Decoder}\left({\alpha}_k\cdot {\beta}_k^S+{\alpha}_k\cdot {\beta}_k^T\right) $$$$ (9)

where $$$ S $$$ denotes source domain and $$$ T $$$ denotes target domain. The decoder preserves the diversity of degradation-specific corrections while maintaining computational sparsity.

The challenge then becomes how to integrate the restored features $$$ {\left\{{R}_k\right\}}_{k\in M} $$$ into a unified representation that balances generalization with domain sensitivity. To address this, we introduce a cross-attention mechanism that adaptively evaluates the contributions of each expert output. Formally, we first project each expert feature $$$ {R}_k $$$ into query, key, and value embeddings along the channel dimension:

$$$$ {Q}_k={W}_q\cdot \mathrm{Pool}\left({R}_k\right),{K}_k={W}_k\cdot \mathrm{Pool}\left({R}_k\right),{V}_k={W}_v\cdot {R}_k $$$$ (10)

where $$$ W $$$ are learnable projection matrices, and $$$ \mathrm{Pool}\left(\cdot \right) $$$ denotes global average pooling that summarizes channel-wise semantics. This design allows attention to be computed across expert channels while preserving spatial information in the value embeddings. The contribution of each expert is then evaluated through a channel-wise cross-attention mechanism:

$$$$ {\gamma}_k=\frac{\exp \left({Q}_k^T{K}_k\right)}{\sum_{k=1}^M\exp \left({Q}_k^T{K}_k\right)} $$$$ (11)

Here, $$$ {\gamma}_k $$$ denotes the normalized attention weight of the k-th expert, reflecting its relevance to the current degradation and domain context. The final unified representation is defined as follow:

$$$$ {R}_{fuse}=\sum \limits_{k=1}^M{\alpha}_k\cdot {V}_k $$$$ (12)

By attending across the channel dimension, the mechanism captures complementary information among the restored features and emphasizes semantically consistent responses while suppressing redundant or conflicting signals. The resulting aggregated representation $$$ {R}_{fuse} $$$ thus inherits the robustness of domain-invariant structures together with the discriminative power of domain-sensitive cues, providing an enhanced feature embedding that is well-suited for downstream detection.

## Abstract

Object detection in adverse weather conditions (e.g., rain, fog, snow) remains a critical challenge due to degraded image quality and semantic ambiguity. Mainstream approaches attempt to bridge this gap by aligning degraded images with their clear counterparts through two main paradigms: cascading enhancer-detector pipelines and multi-task learning frameworks that jointly optimize restoration and detection objectives. However, these methods often fail to reach their full potential due to the disparity between image restoration and detection tasks. Restoration networks prioritize pixel-level fidelity, while detection networks focus on high-level semantic accuracy, leading to suboptimal performance.

## Abstract

Aerial detection requires fine detail for small targets and broad context for large structures. Sampling loss, blur, and noise can weaken detail without changing feature-map resolution, creating a demand–reliability mismatch. We present Physical Imaging Reliability-Guided Scale-Biased Feature Reweighting (PIR-SBFR), a sensor-to-inference interface that separates scene-scale demand from observation reliability. A visual branch estimates demand, while a fixed analytic rule converts relative sampling, sharpness, noise, and field availability into an ordered multiscale prior. The routing rule requires no sensor-specific response fitting. On public DIOR images, ten paired runs increase average precision (AP) from 62.98% (SD 0.32) to 65.52% (SD 0.40). On the study-specific AI-TOD-v2 split, AP rises from 29.58% (SD 0.27) to 32.10% (SD 0.41). Matched local reproductions of three contemporary tiny-object detectors reach 30.42–31.59% AP under the same protocol. PIR-SBFR also improves three compatible host detectors by 2.09–2.54 percentage points. --------------------------------------------------------------------------------

#### Abstract

Salient Object Detection in underwater images remains challenging due to low contrast, uneven illumination, and color distortion caused by scattering and absorption effects, which limit the effectiveness of conventional SOD methods in underwater environments. To address these challenges, we propose a Degradation-aware Conditional Generation Network (DCGNet), specifically designed to construct reliable conditional features for underwater saliency generation. First, we design a Dynamic Multi-Granularity module (DMG) grounded in the human visual system to robustly detect salient objects of varying scales with blurred boundaries. Then, we develop an Underwater Physics-Prior module (UPP), which utilizes pseudo-depth guidance to estimate underwater light attenuation and backscatter, thereby restoring degradation-aware RGB features and mitigating color distortion and boundary ambiguity. --------------------------------------------------------------------------------

#### 3.2.4. Model Lightweight Design

Onboard UAV platforms impose stringent constraints on computing power, memory, and energy budgets, rendering most general-purpose detection networks unsuitable for real-time deployment. Unlike generic lightweight tasks, UAV imagery small-object detection faces a unique and fundamental tension: the compression of lightweight models will lead to loss of fine-grained features of objects. Accordingly, existing studies have gradually formed four main routes: model compression, lightweight structural design, task-specific module optimization, and hardware–software co-deployment.

Model compression reduces network redundancy through knowledge distillation, sparsification, and channel pruning, aiming to minimize performance degradation while lowering complexity. Fan et al. [107] applied distillation atop optimized feature fusion to transfer discriminative knowledge from teacher to student. To reduce the inference burden caused by high-resolution feature preservation, Belmouhcine et al. [108] proposed dual-training distillation where an early-upsampling network guides a late-upsampling detector.
