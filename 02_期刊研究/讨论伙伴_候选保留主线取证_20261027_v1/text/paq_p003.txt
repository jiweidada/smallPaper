3.1. Preliminary
DETR Architecture.A DETR-style detector typically
consists of three components: a convolutional backbone, a
transformer encoder–decoder, and task-specific prediction
heads. Given an input imageI∈R h×w×3, the backbone
extracts image tokensX∈R m×d, which are refined by
the encoder via self-attention to produce contextualized fea-
turesZ=Encoder(X)∈R m×d. The decoder operates on
a set of object queriesQ={q 1, q2, . . . , qn} ∈R n×d, each
of which iteratively attends to image features through self-
and cross-attention acrossLlayers:
Ql =FFN(CrossAttn(SelfAttn(Q l−1),Z)). (1)
The final layer outputQ L is mapped by prediction heads
to class probabilities and bounding boxesY=Head(Q L).
This formulation eliminates non-maximum suppression and
allows end-to-end optimization through bipartite matching,
which forms the basis for our dynamic query design.
One-to-Many Assignment.DETR formulates object de-
tection as a bipartite matching problem, where each ground-
truth object is uniquely paired with a single prediction. The
optimal matchingσis obtained by minimizing a cost that
jointly considers classification and bounding-box regression
through Hungarian algorithm [21]. The one-to-one match-
ing loss is expressed as:
L1:1 =λ 1Lcls(cσ, c∗) +λ2Lbox(bσ, b∗) +λ3Lgiou(bσ, b∗),

andL giou are theL 1 and GIoU losses between the matched
boxesb σ andb ∗, respectively.
